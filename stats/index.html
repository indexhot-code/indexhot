GEO benchmarks: which numbers are worth citing

Last updated November 26, 2026

Most statistics in this field cannot be traced to a sample. This page separates what we counted ourselves from what published research measured, and states plainly what we decline to publish because no reproducible source exists.

What our own panel measures

Every figure we report comes from one governed run. The parameters are fixed so that a later run is comparable rather than merely similar.

Parameter Value Why it is set this way
Brands 20 Wide enough to compare categories, narrow enough to hand-code absorption
Prompts 50 per brand, 10 branded and 40 non-branded Forty non-branded across eight clusters is the minimum for a cluster-level read
Surfaces ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini; AI Overviews scored separately Three chat surfaces plus one search surface, because their attribution behaviour differs
Runs 3 per prompt per surface, median coded Single runs cannot separate a change from sampling noise
Answers analysed 3,000+ The figure quoted in the site header
Period 2026 Q3 Stated because model versions move and an undated rate implies permanence
Coding Manual, two coders, 20 answers calibrated first Absorption is judgement, and uncalibrated judgement drifts

Any rate quoted from this panel without those parameters is not a figure we can defend, and we will say so if asked.

What public research actually shows

Two published studies are worth citing in this field, and both state their samples.

Study Sample Finding
GEO: Generative Engine Optimization Benchmarked optimisation methods across generative engine responses Visibility gains of up to 40 percent for optimised content, with the effect varying by method and query type
From Citation Selection to Citation Absorption 602 prompts, 21,143 citations, 18,151 fetched pages Being cited and being used are separate outcomes, with a large share of cited pages contributing nothing to the final answer
AMEC GEO hub Industry measurement framework, not a sample Proposes standard definitions for AI visibility measurement; useful for terminology, not for rates

The second study is the reason this site reports absorption separately from citation. It is the only published work we have found that measures the difference at scale.

Three traps in every GEO statistic

  1. No denominator. A percentage of what, across how many prompts, in which version of which model. A rate without a sample reads as a fact and behaves as a rumour.
  2. Version mixing. Numbers collected across a model release are not comparable, and the discontinuity is invisible unless the collection date is stated. Every rate on this site carries a period for this reason.
  3. Surface mixing. Citation counts are not comparable between a surface that attaches twelve sources and one that attaches two. Aggregating them produces a figure that describes neither.

What we decline to publish

Three categories of number are common in this field and absent here, because we could not reproduce them from a stated source.

  • Single-figure claims about what share of search traffic AI answers now take. Definitions differ, panels differ, and no method is published with the estimate.
  • Category-level averages presented as benchmarks, when the underlying sample per category is a handful of answers.
  • Growth projections. A projection is a scenario, and dressing it as measurement is the failure this page exists to avoid.

Where a category in our panel has fewer than forty relevant answers, we say so on the page rather than rounding to a confident-looking figure. That limitation is the honest part of the dataset.

How to cite these numbers

Quote the rate, the sample and the period together. The BibTeX entry for the dataset is on the data page; the measurement definitions are in the standard.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there no single figure for AI search traffic share?

Because no public source measures it consistently. Estimates differ by definition, by surface and by panel, and none publishes a reproducible method, so we do not repeat one.

Can I reuse your panel figures?

Yes, with attribution and the sample size. The panel is 20 brands, 50 prompts, three surfaces, 2026 Q3, and any figure quoted without that context is not our claim.

Are these numbers a forecast?

No. They describe what was counted in a defined sample in a defined period. Nothing here predicts future visibility.
