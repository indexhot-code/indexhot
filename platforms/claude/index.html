Platform guide · Claude

How to appear in Claude's answers

Claude answers read like an analyst's brief: longer, hedged, and explicit about uncertainty. Citations are rarer and more consequential than on surfaces that attach a long source list, which makes each one worth more and harder to earn.

What answers look like here

Responses tend to be structured prose with caveats attached: a recommendation followed by the conditions under which it holds. Claude is more likely than other surfaces to say that the answer depends, and to describe two options rather than pick one.

That shape rewards pages that state conditions explicitly. A page saying this works up to a certain seat count, beyond which it does not, gives the model something to hedge around. A page that only claims superiority gives it nothing to qualify.

  • Conditions and limits are quoted, because they support the hedging the model prefers.
  • Method and provenance are discussed more often than price, relative to other surfaces.
  • Long, well-structured technical documentation is retrieved more readily than marketing pages.

How it chooses what to cite

Retrieval behaviour here favours documents with clear internal structure and self-contained sections, and it draws heavily on technical and reference material: documentation, specifications, changelogs, and substantive essays.

Citations appear in fewer answers than on surfaces with a permanent source panel, and where they appear they tend to mark the page that supplied the dominant framing rather than one of twelve equal links. That makes presence binary in a useful way: either you shaped the answer or you did not.

The practical implication is that a single well-structured page can outperform a large content programme here, because the unit being retrieved is the passage and the competition per passage is thinner.

Where brands go missing

  • Marketing pages with no technical substance, which have nothing the model can qualify or repeat.
  • Documentation that exists only as a rendered app with no static HTML.
  • Pages that hedge so heavily that no claim survives extraction, leaving the model nothing to cite.

The second failure is the most common in developer-facing categories: the docs are excellent, they live behind a client-side renderer, and no retrieval pipeline can read them.

A five-step checklist

  1. State the condition, not only the benefit: what the product is for, at what scale, and where it stops working.
  2. Publish documentation as static HTML with headings that name their subject.
  3. Put the specification in text, with units, rather than in an image or a table exported as an image.
  4. Give each section a claim in its first sentence, so a passage survives being cut out.
  5. Check what the surface says about you now, and record it before changing anything.

How we measure presence here

It is scored as one of four surfaces with its own weight, using the same fifty prompts and the same coding rules. Because citations are scarcer here, a citation on this surface is reported separately rather than averaged into a combined figure, where it would be swamped by surfaces that attach more sources.

  • Mention, citation and absorption coded separately, as on every surface.
  • Runs dated, because this surface changes with model releases more visibly than others.
  • Absorption weighted highest, since the surface elaborates rather than lists.

What usually fails here

Teams optimise for volume and see nothing move, because this surface rewards one excellent passage over ten adequate pages. The second common failure is treating a hedged answer as a loss: the model saying it depends, and naming you as one of the things it depends on, is a win.

The third is measuring on a prompt set built for consumer categories. Technical and B2B questions are where this surface is strongest, and a library of shopping prompts will report near-zero presence that says nothing about the brand's actual position.

Frequently asked questions

Does Claude cite sources?

Sometimes, and less consistently than surfaces with a permanent source panel. When it does, the cited page usually supplied the framing of the answer rather than one of many links.

Is documentation worth more than marketing here?

Generally yes. Substance is what can be qualified and repeated; positioning claims rarely are.

Should I weight this surface differently?

Yes. Score it separately and then combine with an explicit weight, rather than averaging raw rates across surfaces that behave differently.
