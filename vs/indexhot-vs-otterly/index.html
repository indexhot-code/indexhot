IndexHot vs Otterly: Which Fits Your GEO Measurement?

By IndexHot Team · November 16, 2026

Otterly is built for monitoring at scale; IndexHot is built for measurement you can reproduce. If you need a dashboard covering several brands by the end of the week, Otterly is the faster route. If you need to prove that a citation changed an answer and show your method to someone who will check it, that is the problem this standard exists for.

Where Otterly is the better choice

If you are an agency running visibility checks for a dozen clients, the operational cost of doing it by hand is the binding constraint, not methodological purity. Otterly is built for exactly that shape of work: multiple brands, scheduled runs, and reports you can hand over without assembling them yourself.

It also wins on continuity. A dashboard that has been collecting for a year gives you a trend line, and a trend line is often more persuasive to a client than a more rigorous single snapshot. Reporting cadence and history are real advantages, not marketing ones.

Where IndexHot is different

The difference is not coverage, it is what happens after the numbers appear. Our measurement fixes the prompt library, records the platform and date of each run, and codes absorption against a published four-level rubric, so a third party can re-run the sample and check the coding.

That matters when someone asks how you know. A dashboard score invites the question 'compared to what, measured how'. A published method with an exported coded sample answers it before it is asked, and that is the only reason we built a standard rather than a product.

How to decide in ten minutes

Answer three questions. Do you need to track more than three brands continuously? Do you need a trend line longer than six months? Do you need to defend the number to an external auditor or a sceptical client?

More than one yes on the first two points toward monitoring tooling. A yes on the third points toward a published method, because reproducibility is not a feature you can add later to a score that was never documented.

In practice the two coexist: teams monitor continuously and audit annually or at the point of a big content investment. That is a reasonable division of labour rather than a compromise.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Otterly and IndexHot together?

Yes, and it is a common arrangement. Monitoring gives you coverage and cadence; a published method gives you something to audit and cite. The only cost is keeping the prompt sets aligned so the two do not disagree for the wrong reason.

Does IndexHot replace monitoring tools?

No. There is no dashboard, no API and no alerting. What exists is a standard, a prompt discipline and a free manual report for the first twenty brands, which is a different product category.

Which is better for an agency?

For many clients at once, monitoring tooling is usually cheaper per brand. For defending a recommendation to a client who asks how the number was produced, a documented method is easier to stand behind.
