What actually changes by industry

The mechanics are identical across categories; what differs is which content shape wins and where the facts live.

In SaaS and agencies the cited pages are comparisons and documentation, and entity ambiguity is the usual blocker because product names are often run as separate companies. In e-commerce and manufacturing the facts sit in specifications, so the win comes from converting images and PDFs into text with units.

Healthcare, legal and finance share a different constraint: the sources winning their answers are institutions and regulators, so a brand page is rarely cited no matter how well written. The workable route is dated, approved facts — schedules, eligibility rules, coverage limits — stated plainly enough to be retrievable.

Education sits in between: accreditation and outcomes are specific enough to be cited, but they are usually buried behind enquiry forms, which is why providers are described by directory listings rather than by their own pages.

How these pages were built

Each page follows the same structure so the differences between industries are comparable rather than anecdotal.

The data section states what the 3,000-answer sample shows for that category, the action plan states what to change, and the verification section states what would count as evidence that it worked. Numbers are drawn from the same run, which is why they can be compared across pages.

Where a category has fewer than forty relevant answers in the sample, we say so on the page rather than implying a precision the data does not support. That is the main limitation of industry-level reporting and it is worth stating in the page itself.
