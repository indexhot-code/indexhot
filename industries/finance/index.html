Industry guide · Finance

AI search visibility for finance brands

Financial queries combine high commercial value with strict regulatory scrutiny. Assistants favour sources that state figures with dates, disclose methodology and carry clear organisational accountability — vague marketing language is filtered out entirely.

Numbers with dates win

Finance is a facts-first category. Rates, fees, eligibility and timelines are the substance of the query, and a model cannot safely repeat a number it cannot source and date. Pages that present figures as plain text with an explicit as-of date are cited far more often than narrative pages.

  • Explicit as-of dates. Undated rates are treated as unreliable.
  • Disclosed methodology. How a figure was calculated is itself a trust signal.
  • Regulatory identity. Licence numbers and registering bodies stated on the page.

Where finance brands lose the answer

  • Fee tables published as PDFs or images instead of HTML.
  • Product pages that describe benefits but omit eligibility and risk.
  • Calculators with no explanatory text for the model to quote.
  • No author or compliance reviewer attached to regulated content.

Finance action plan

  1. Publish every rate and fee as HTML text with a visible as-of date.
  2. Attach a named author and compliance reviewer to regulated pages.
  3. State eligibility, risk and jurisdiction alongside product benefits.
  4. Add explanatory copy around every calculator so it can be summarised.
  5. Keep regulatory registrations consistent across site and directories.

What the data shows

Financial questions produce the most hedged answers we measure. Because answers must avoid advice, they cite regulators, official filings and established financial media, and they mention brands with visible qualification, often noting fees, restrictions or risk warnings. A brand can be mentioned in a large share of relevant answers while every mention carries a caveat, which is why sentiment and absorption matter more here than raw visibility.

How to verify it worked

Ask five product-comparison questions and read the full answer, including qualifiers. Score two things: whether you are named and whether the answer attaches a limitation to you that is factually wrong. A wrong limitation is a correction task, not a content task. After publishing dated fee schedules and eligibility rules, re-run the same questions in six weeks and check whether the qualifier changed, not just whether the mention count did.

The questions buyers actually ask

These five prompts are a usable starting library for financial services buyers. Ask them verbatim across all four surfaces and record mention, citation and absorption per prompt before changing anything.

  • Which account has the lowest fees for this use case?
  • What are the eligibility rules for this product?
  • How does this compare with the market leader on cost?
  • What happens to this rate if the base rate changes?
  • Which provider offers this service in this country?

Where teams get stuck

The recurring problem is that compliance forbids the specific claim the answer needs. Pages hedge, answers hedge further, and readers end up with a comparison that names nobody. The workable path is to publish dated, approved facts: fee schedules, eligibility tables, regulatory status. Specificity that has passed review is the only content that survives a hedged category.

Frequently asked questions

Why are financial pages rarely cited by AI?

Often because key figures are locked in PDFs or images and carry no date. Models avoid repeating numbers they cannot source and timestamp.

Do compliance disclosures help AI visibility?

They help trust evaluation. Clear regulatory identity and disclosed methodology are signals that a source is safe to ground on.

Should I publish rate tables as HTML?

Yes. Machine-readable HTML tables with as-of dates are among the most citable assets in finance.
