Industry guide · E-commerce

AI search visibility for e-commerce brands

AI assistants now answer “what should I buy” questions directly, and they quote product pages that expose specifications, price and availability as plain facts. Retailers whose catalogue data lives only in JavaScript widgets or image galleries are effectively invisible.

What changes when the shelf becomes a sentence

In classic search, a product page competes for a click. In AI answers, the product is described, compared and recommended inside the response. That shifts the unit of competition from the page to the product record: title, specs, price, rating and availability all have to be machine-readable.

  • Specification completeness. Missing dimensions, materials or compatibility rules remove you from filtered recommendations.
  • Price transparency. Models avoid recommending products whose price cannot be stated.
  • Review depth. Aggregated ratings with volume beat a handful of testimonials.

The catalogue gaps that cost sales

  • Specs rendered in tabs or accordions that never appear in the HTML source.
  • Variant confusion: one URL for twelve sizes, with no structured variant data.
  • Category pages that list products but never explain how to choose between them.
  • No buying-guide content, so the model sources advice from marketplaces instead.

E-commerce action plan

  1. Render core specs, price and availability in server-side HTML.
  2. Add Product markup with offers, ratings and variants.
  3. Publish one buying guide per category that explains trade-offs explicitly.
  4. Consolidate variant URLs with canonical tags.
  5. Track which competitors are named for your top twenty commercial prompts.

What the data shows

Product queries in our sample show a consistent split: the answer describes the category confidently and names a marketplace or a review site, while individual store pages are cited rarely. Where a merchant does earn citations, the cited page is almost always a buying guide or a specification table rather than a product listing. The most common failure we see is specifications published only as images inside a listing, which makes them unreadable to retrieval and invisible to absorption scoring.

How to verify it worked

Take five products and ask an assistant to compare three options in the category. Note whether your brand appears, whether your domain is cited, and whether any figure in the answer matches your listed specification. Then publish one specification table as HTML text with a visible date and repeat the same five questions after three weeks. A single correct figure reproduced in an answer is a stronger signal than ten extra mentions.

The questions buyers actually ask

These five prompts are a usable starting library for e-commerce buyers. Ask them verbatim across all four surfaces and record mention, citation and absorption per prompt before changing anything.

  • Which espresso machine under 500 dollars is most reliable?
  • How does this model compare with the previous generation?
  • What are the actual dimensions and weight of this product?
  • Which store ships replacement filters fastest?
  • Is this compatible with the older accessory line?

Where teams get stuck

Merchants typically lose on three fronts. Specifications live in images, so retrieval cannot read them. Buying guides are written for the brand rather than for the comparison being asked. And product variants share near-duplicate pages that split whatever citation authority exists, which is a canonical problem masquerading as a content problem.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI assistants recommend my products directly?

Yes. Assistants describe and compare products inside the answer. They favour pages where specifications, price and availability are available as plain, structured facts.

Does Product schema help with AI shopping answers?

It materially improves eligibility and fact extraction, especially for price, rating and availability, but it does not replace clear on-page specification text.

Why do marketplaces get recommended instead of my store?

Marketplaces usually expose cleaner, more complete product data and aggregate reviews. If your catalogue hides specs or price, the model defaults to the source it can read.
