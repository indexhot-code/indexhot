Industry guide · Agencies

AI search visibility for agencies

Agency selection queries are trust queries: buyers ask assistants to name credible providers for a specific service and market. Winning that answer requires specific service pages, published case evidence and third-party corroboration — a broad “full-service” homepage persuades nobody, least of all a model.

Specificity beats breadth

“Full-service digital agency” is a phrase that carries no retrievable meaning. Models match a query to a service, a sector and a geography. An agency with twelve narrow service pages is far more likely to be named than one with a single broad page, even if the broad page ranks better in classic search.

  • Service + sector pages. “GEO for B2B SaaS” is retrievable; “digital growth” is not.
  • Case evidence with numbers. Specific outcomes, named clients where permitted.
  • Named practitioners. Who actually does the work, with credentials.

Why agencies get skipped

  • No case studies with quantified results.
  • Service pages that list deliverables instead of outcomes.
  • Anonymous team pages with stock photography and no bylines.
  • No comparison or “how we differ” content in a crowded market.

Agency action plan

  1. Split services into narrow, retrievable pages by service and sector.
  2. Publish three case studies with before-and-after numbers.
  3. Add named authors with credentials and contact details.
  4. State pricing model or engagement minimums in text.
  5. Earn third-party mentions in the directories buyers actually read.

What the data shows

Agency questions behave differently from product questions: buyers ask who is good at a specific problem, and answers assemble the list from rankings, directories, client testimonials and case studies published elsewhere. An agency's own site is cited most often for a methodology or process claim, and least often for its own results claims, which are treated as unverifiable self-reporting.

How to verify it worked

Ask five problem-led questions, such as who handles retention for subscription businesses. Record whether you are named at all, then whether any answer cites your site. The realistic first win is being named without being cited, produced by third-party lists rather than on-site edits. After that, publish one methodology page with concrete process steps and one case study with a verifiable metric, and re-test in six weeks.

The questions buyers actually ask

These five prompts are a usable starting library for agency buyers. Ask them verbatim across all four surfaces and record mention, citation and absorption per prompt before changing anything.

  • Which agency specialises in retention for subscription businesses?
  • Who has experience with this ad platform at this spend level?
  • What does a typical engagement cost for this scope?
  • Which agency has published case studies in this vertical?
  • Who works with clients of this size?

Where teams get stuck

Agencies are named by directories and rankings, not by their own sites, because results claims read as unverifiable. Two fixes matter more than new landing pages: publishing methodology with concrete process steps, which answers are willing to cite, and earning placement on the lists that already supply these answers.

Frequently asked questions

How do agencies get recommended by AI assistants?

Through narrow, retrievable service pages backed by quantified case evidence and third-party corroboration. Broad positioning language is rarely cited.

Do case studies matter for AI visibility?

Yes. Specific outcomes with numbers give the model a concrete claim it can repeat, which is exactly what recommendation answers need.

Should an agency publish pricing?

At minimum publish the model or engagement minimum. Without any pricing signal, assistants often skip the agency for budget-sensitive queries.
