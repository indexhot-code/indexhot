Industry guide · wholesale and distribution

AI search visibility for wholesale and distribution

Distribution sells availability and terms, and both are structured data that most distributors publish badly: a catalogue behind a login, a price list as a PDF, and MOQ explained in an email. The answer surfaces have no way to use any of it.

What buyers ask a distributor

QuestionWhat the answer needs
minimum order for this component
MOQ per SKU and per order, with a date

Every one of these is a lookup, not an argument. That is the structural difference from B2C ecommerce, where persuasion matters: here the winning page is the one that answers lookups accurately and dates them.

What gets cited here

  • Catalogue pages with SKU, description, MOQ and tier pricing in HTML.
  • Availability statements with a warehouse and a timestamp.
  • Incoterm and shipping terms stated per region rather than in a policy page.
  • Cross-reference tables mapping your part numbers to the ones buyers already use.

The cross-reference table is the most underrated asset in distribution. Buyers ask by the incumbent's part number, and a mapping page answers a question your own catalogue navigation cannot.

Where distributors go missing

  • Login-gated catalogues. The entire product data set unavailable to retrieval.
  • PDF price lists. Tiered pricing that no pipeline can lift into an answer.
  • No cross-reference content. Buyers search a competitor's part number and never reach you.
  • Availability without a timestamp. A stock figure with no time is not a fact.

Five changes that move this sector

  1. Publish a public catalogue subset in HTML: SKU, description, MOQ, tier boundaries.
  2. Put price tiers on the page with currency and incoterm, even if the exact figure is a range.
  3. Build cross-reference pages from the mappings your sales team already maintains.
  4. Timestamp availability and say which warehouse it refers to.
  5. State drop-ship and returns conditions in one sentence per region, not in a linked policy.

How we measure this sector

Prompts are lookup-shaped and include competitor part numbers, which is how real buyers search. Reporting the branded half separately matters here more than elsewhere, because distributors often rank well on their own name and not at all on the part numbers that generate the order.

Frequently asked questions

Is gating the catalogue a mistake?

For the whole catalogue, yes. Gate the net prices and account-specific terms, publish the SKUs, MOQ and tier structure.

Do part-number cross references really help?

They capture queries you cannot win on brand, and they make you the page that resolves the equivalence.

Should availability be public?

A dated, warehouse-specific figure is defensible. An undated one invites a support ticket.
