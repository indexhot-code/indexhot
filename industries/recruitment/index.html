GEO for recruitment firms and employers

Last updated November 2026

Hiring questions have moved into answer surfaces on both sides of the market: candidates ask what a role pays and how a process works, employers ask what a search costs and how long it takes. Both are answered from pages that state numbers, and both punish sites that keep everything behind a form.

What the answers look like here

Candidate prompts are specific about money and process: what a role in a given city pays, how long a company's interview process takes, whether remote hiring is possible. Employer prompts are about cost and speed. Each answer names two or three firms or employers and gives a range or a timeline.

Salary data is the citable asset. A firm publishing dated ranges per role per region is quoted constantly; one publishing competitive salary is invisible in exactly the queries that matter.

  • Salary bands dated to a quarter are absorbed; undated bands read as stale within months.
  • Process descriptions with stage counts and timelines are quoted because they resolve anxiety.
  • Expired listings left live poison the whole cluster, teaching crawlers that the site's dates are unreliable.

Where visibility breaks

  • Every role is a live listing that expires, so the site has no stable page to cite.
  • Salary is described as competitive, so the model uses a job board's estimate instead.
  • Employer brand and recruitment firm are described in the same words, so answers attribute one to the other.

The expiry problem is unique here. Most sites accumulate dead listings, and a cluster of pages saying this role is closed is a poor signal for every other page in the same section.

A practical sequence

  1. Publish dated salary bands per role per region and refresh them quarterly with a visible date.
  2. Create evergreen role pages describing the shape of the job, the process and the timeline, separate from live listings.
  3. Remove or redirect expired listings within a week, with a 410 or a 301 to the role family page.
  4. Keep employer and agency entities distinct in schema and prose, and state the relationship explicitly.
  5. Run candidate and employer prompt sets separately, because the two audiences fail differently.

How to verify it worked

Check salary absorption specifically: does the answer use your band or a job board's? The second outcome sends candidates into a conversation armed with the wrong number.

  • Track the ratio of live to expired URLs monthly; it predicts how much crawl is wasted.
  • Re-run salary prompts each quarter after refreshing the bands, and note whether the cited range moved with yours.

Frequently asked questions

Should we publish salary bands competitors do not?

Yes, with ranges and dates. The band is the reason a candidate opens a conversation; withholding it moves the conversation to a job board.

How do we handle expired roles?

Redirect to the role family page or return 410 within a week. Leaving them live with a closed notice is the worst option for crawl efficiency.

Does this apply to in-house HR teams?

Yes, on the employer side: process length, remote policy and salary transparency are the citable facts, and they are what candidates ask about.
