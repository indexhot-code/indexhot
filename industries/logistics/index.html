Industry guide · logistics and freight

AI search visibility for logistics and freight

Freight questions are operational: transit days, cut-offs, documentation, liability. Answers are built from schedules, rate tables and authority notices, so the asset that wins is a table a machine can read, not a page of capability prose.

What buyers actually ask

Question shapeWhat the answer must contain
transit time from Shanghai to Rotterdam
Days, mode, whether it is port-to-port or door-to-door

Every one of these is answerable from a structured table and unanswerable from a paragraph about end-to-end solutions. That is the core finding in this sector, and it explains why well-written logistics sites lose to thinner pages that happen to publish numbers.

What gets cited here

  • Lane tables with transit days, cut-offs and mode, dated.
  • Documentation checklists that name the issuer and the sequence.
  • Rate ranges with the basis stated, rather than request-a-quote forms.
  • Authority and port notices, which are treated as primary sources.
  • Incident and congestion updates, because they are time-sensitive and verifiable.

The pattern is consistent: the cited source is the one that supplied a figure with a date. Carriers with PDF schedules and no HTML equivalent are structurally invisible, because the retrieval step cannot open the PDF's table in a form it can use.

Where freight sites go missing

  • Quote gates. The transit table sits behind a form, so the page that would answer the question contains no answer.
  • PDF-only schedules. Excellent operations data, published in a format retrieval cannot parse.
  • No lane pages. One page describing global coverage instead of forty pages, one per lane, each with a table.
  • Undated figures. A transit time with no month is unusable on a route where schedules change quarterly.

Five changes that move this sector

  1. Publish one page per major lane with a dated transit table in HTML.
  2. State the basis of every figure: port-to-port or door-to-door, business days or calendar days.
  3. Turn the documentation checklist into a table with issuer and timing per document.
  4. Give rate ranges with the assumptions, rather than a form and nothing else.
  5. Add a visible schedule-updated date and refresh it when the schedule changes, not when the site deploys.

How we measure this sector

Prompts are written as operational questions with a lane, a volume or a document, because that is how the buying happens. Seasonality is recorded alongside, since a peak-season answer and an off-peak answer are not comparable, and reporting them as one number hides the only insight worth having.

Frequently asked questions

Are PDF schedules really invisible?

Largely. Retrieval works on HTML text; a scanned or table-heavy PDF contributes little, and often nothing beyond its title.

Should lane pages be one per route?

Yes for major lanes. It gives each route a page whose table answers one question completely, which is what passage retrieval selects.

Does pricing have to be public?

Ranges with stated assumptions work. A form-only page supplies nothing to quote and earns no citation.
