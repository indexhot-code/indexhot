GEO for professional services firms

Last updated November 2026

Professional services buyers now ask AI systems for a shortlist before they contact a firm. The answers quote fee structures, engagement models and credentials, so a firm that publishes none of those is structurally absent rather than outcompeted.

What the answers look like here

Prompts in this vertical are unusually specific about money and process: what a statutory audit costs for a 40-person company, how long a transfer pricing review takes, whether a fractional CFO handles payroll. Each expects a range, a timeline or a scope boundary, and each is answered with a paragraph naming two or three firms and describing how they work.

That is a different retrieval target from a services homepage. The homepage says what the firm believes; the answer needs what the engagement contains. With no page stating scope, duration and fee band, the model falls back to directories, association listings and competitors that do say it.

  • Fee ranges published as text are quoted far more often than contact us for a quote.
  • Credential pages naming the qualification, the regulator and the year read as evidence; a logo wall does not.
  • Case descriptions with a number in them get absorbed; helped a client grow does not.

Where visibility breaks

  • Every case study is a gated PDF, so the substance exists and no pipeline can read it.
  • Pricing is absent by policy, so the model substitutes a competitor's published range or a directory estimate.
  • Partner biographies name people but never state what they are qualified to sign off, which is the fact buyers search.

The pattern is deliberate opacity that made sense when the only channel was a referral. On an answer surface, opacity is not discretion; it is an empty slot that somebody else fills.

A practical sequence

  1. Publish one page per service with scope, typical duration and a fee range, even if wide. A range beats silence.
  2. Convert three case studies from PDF to HTML with the sector, the problem, the intervention and one measurable outcome.
  3. Give each partner a page stating qualification, regulator, years and the service lines they own.
  4. Add Organization and Person schema, with sameAs links to professional registers and LinkedIn.
  5. Run fifty prompts across discovery, pricing and credentials, and record which firms appear instead of yours.

How to verify it worked

Measure two things separately: whether the firm is named at all, and whether your fee range or duration survives into the answer. The second is the commercial signal, because it means the buyer arrives pre-qualified on price.

  • Track absorption of your published range: does the answer repeat your band or a competitor's?
  • Re-run the pricing cluster monthly; fee pages decay faster than credential pages.

Frequently asked questions

Can a firm publish fee ranges without losing negotiating room?

Yes, and wide ranges are the norm here. A published band filters unqualified enquiries rather than setting the price.

How is this different from legal?

Legal buyers ask about outcomes and process. Professional services buyers ask about scope, credentials and fee structure, so the retrievable facts differ.

What if advertising the service is restricted?

Publishing factual service descriptions, credentials and fee bands is usually permissible where persuasive claims are not. The constraint affects persuasion, not information.
