Industry guide · insurance

AI search visibility for insurance

Insurance answers are hedged, because coverage depends on wording. That makes definitions, exclusions and jurisdiction the quotable material, and it makes regulator and aggregator sources the strongest competitors for the sentence.

What buyers ask, and how answers hedge

Question shapeTypical answer shape
does home insurance cover burst pipes
Yes if the policy includes escape of water; exclusions for maintenance

The hedging is not evasion. Coverage genuinely depends on the wording, and an answer that asserts without qualification is a liability. This is the one sector where a hedged answer is the correct answer, which changes what absorption means here.

Who gets cited, and why

  • Regulators and official guidance, which carry authority a brand cannot match.
  • Aggregators and comparison sites, which hold structured, comparable facts.
  • Broker explainers that define terms in plain language and state exclusions.
  • Policy documents, when the wording is published as HTML rather than as a PDF.

The consequence: in most coverage questions you are competing not for the recommendation but for the definition. A brand that supplies the clearest plain-language definition of escape of water is more likely to be the source than a brand that supplies another landing page about protection.

Where insurers go missing

  • Policy wording as PDF. The exact quotable material, published in the one format retrieval cannot lift.
  • No exclusion pages. Cover is described and exclusions are buried, so the answer hedges and cites a regulator instead.
  • Jurisdiction silence. A definition valid in one market presented as universal, which makes it unsafe to repeat.
  • Aggregator dependency. Facts live only on comparison sites, so the aggregator earns the citation.

Five changes that move this sector

  1. Publish definitions and exclusions as HTML pages, one concept per page, in plain language.
  2. State the jurisdiction and the policy class in the first sentence of every coverage statement.
  3. Keep your own comparable facts on your own site, so aggregators are not the only structured source.
  4. Date the wording: a definition tied to a policy year is safer to quote than an undated one.
  5. Give the negative case its own section, since the qualifier is what the answer needs.

How we measure this sector

Prompts split by jurisdiction, because a definition that is correct in one market is wrong in another and blending them produces a number that means nothing. Absorption is coded for the qualifier as well as the claim: an answer that repeats your definition with your exclusion attached is a stronger outcome than one that repeats the definition alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is hedging a bad outcome here?

No. In regulated categories, an answer that names you as the source of a qualification is a win, because the qualification is the useful part.

Do comparison sites always win?

They win the structured-fact prompts. You can still win the definitional ones, which is where plain-language pages beat everyone.

Should policy wording be public?

The parts that answer common questions, yes. Publishing definitions and exclusions as HTML is the single highest-leverage change in this sector.
