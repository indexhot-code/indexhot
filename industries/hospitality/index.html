GEO for hotels, restaurants and travel operators

Last updated November 2026

Travel is one of the most AI-mediated categories there is: itineraries, hotel shortlists and restaurant picks are routinely assembled by an answer rather than a results page. The winners are not the properties with the best website but the ones whose facts, rates and policies are readable as text.

What the answers look like here

Prompts combine constraints: a budget, a party size, a location and a requirement. Family hotel near the old town under 150 euros with parking expects a short list with a reason per property, and the reason is always a specific attribute: a distance in metres, a policy, a rate, a capacity.

That makes machine-readable attributes the whole game. A property whose site says ideally located but never states the distance, the parking fee or the child policy contributes nothing citable, however good the photography is.

  • Distances in minutes or metres are quoted; conveniently located is discarded.
  • Policy pages for cancellation, children, pets and late check-in are high value because they resolve a constraint.
  • Rate ranges dated to a season survive; undated prices are treated as stale.

Where visibility breaks

  • Rates and availability live behind a booking engine, so the extractable page has no numbers.
  • OTA listings outrank the property's own site because they carry the structured attributes the site lacks.
  • Opening hours and seasonal closures sit in an image or a footer, invisible to extraction.

The commercial sting is that the OTA becomes the source of truth about your own property, including descriptions and policy interpretations you did not write.

A practical sequence

  1. Publish a rate range per room type per season as text, dated, next to the booking link.
  2. State the five attributes that resolve constraints: distance to centre or station, parking cost, child policy, cancellation terms, check-in window.
  3. Convert one page per property into a structure with Hotel or Restaurant schema, priceRange and amenity lists.
  4. Write the local-intent pages: what is within walking distance, how to arrive, what is closed on which day.
  5. Run prompts that include a constraint rather than only a destination, and check whether your property is named and your rate repeated.

How to verify it worked

Check whether your rate band and policy statements appear, not only whether the property is named. A named property with a competitor's price attached is worse than absence, because the buyer arrives expecting a rate you will not honour.

  • Track rate absorption monthly; seasonality makes the window short and stale rates decay fast.
  • Watch which OTA is cited alongside your name; a citation through an OTA is a partial win with a commission attached.

Frequently asked questions

Do OTAs have an advantage we cannot match?

They have structured data. That advantage is replicable: publish the same attributes on your own domain with the same clarity.

Should we publish rates that change constantly?

Publish dated ranges rather than exact nightly figures. A range dated to a season stays accurate long enough to be useful.

Does this apply to restaurants?

Yes, with different attributes: hours, cuisine, price band, booking policy and dietary handling are the citable facts.
