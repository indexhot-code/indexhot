Why the gap matters more than either rate

Branded mention rate tells you whether the answer engine knows you exist. Unbranded rate tells you whether it thinks of you when the buyer does not name you. The second is the one that decides whether AI search sends you anyone new.

Reporting the two separately is the point. A single blended visibility score hides the only distinction that changes what you do next: one number improved by mentioning the brand more often, the other only by being the best answer to a category question.

What the three bands mean in practice

  • Under 15 points. Category recall is keeping pace with branded recall. Protect it: fresh dates, stable URLs, and no redesign of the pages that are already winning.
  • 15 to 40 points. The normal shape for most brands, and the one worth working on. Unbranded gaps are usually evidence gaps, not vocabulary gaps.
  • Above 40 points. Effectively invisible outside branded prompts. Check indexability and category vocabulary before commissioning any new pages, because a 40-point gap is rarely an editorial failure.

The counter at the bottom is not a target, it is an ordering device. If closing the gap needs twenty more of forty prompts to change, that is a category position, not a page. If it needs three, the fix is likely one page and one passage.

Three ways this number gets misused

First, comparing gaps across categories. A crowded category will show a wider gap than a narrow one for identical quality of work, because there are more competitors to be named instead of you.

Second, treating the gap as a trend from a single run. Answer engines vary run to run; a gap measured once is a photograph, not a direction. Three runs minimum, and record the dates.

Third, weighting branded prompts into the total to make the headline rate look better. Keep the two groups separate in every report, and let the gap be the finding rather than a footnote.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a branded prompt?

Any prompt that contains your brand or product name. Everything else is unbranded, including category questions, comparison questions and problem-first questions.

How many prompts do I need?

Enough that one answer changing does not move the rate by more than a few points. Ten branded and forty unbranded is a workable starting shape, not a standard.

Should I include the same prompts on every platform?

Yes, and report each platform separately. A blended rate across surfaces hides which engine is actually carrying you.
