Citation Extractability Checker

Paste the opening of a page section. The checker estimates whether an answer engine could lift a usable claim from it.

A summary has to compress. It can only use passages that state a complete point without requiring the surrounding argument, so pages that build to a conclusion over several paragraphs contribute nothing. This checker reads text you paste and flags the four properties that decide survival.

Paste 80 to 600 words, ideally starting at a heading. Nothing leaves your browser.

    First thing to fix

    The four checks

    Each check maps to a failure we see repeatedly in pages that rank well and are still never quoted.

    • Answer first. The opening sentence should carry the claim. Context before the answer usually means the passage is unusable.
    • A checkable fact. Numbers, thresholds, units or dates give a summary something concrete to keep.
    • Condition with the figure. A number separated from its condition becomes wrong when lifted, so engines avoid it.
    • Sentence length. Long, clause-heavy sentences get trimmed in ways that change meaning, so shorter sentences survive intact.

    A worked example

    The same fact, written two ways. The first version is competent prose that a summary cannot use; the second is the same information in a form that survives compression.

    Before

    “In today’s fast-moving logistics landscape, speed has become a defining competitive advantage. Our fulfilment network has been built over many years with significant investment, and the result is that customers across Europe now benefit from noticeably quicker delivery than was previously possible, which we believe reflects our commitment to operational excellence.”

    Unusable: 92 words, no figure, no condition, claim arrives only at the end as a belief rather than a fact.

    After

    “Orders placed before 15:00 CET ship the same day from our Rotterdam and Poznan hubs; orders placed after 15:00 ship the next working day. Median transit time to EU destinations was 1.8 days in 2025, measured across 41,000 shipments.”

    Usable: two sentences, a threshold with its condition, a median with a unit, a sample size and a year.

    The rewrite did not add information. Every number in the second version was already known to the team that wrote the first. What changed is that the fact now sits in the sentence with its conditions, so a summary that lifts one line still states something true.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Does my text get sent anywhere?

    No. The analysis runs entirely in your browser with regular expressions and word counts. There is no request, no storage and no logging, which is why the tool works offline.

    Is a high score a guarantee of citation?

    No. Extractability is one condition among several, alongside eligibility, entity clarity and access. A page can be perfectly liftable and still never be retrieved, in which case the problem is upstream.

    Should I rewrite a whole page to score higher?

    Usually not. Move the claim into the first sentence of each section and keep the explanation after it. That single change lifts extractability on most pages without touching what the page actually argues.
