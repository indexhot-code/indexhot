Reporting AI Visibility: Direct Answers

7 questions · answered directly

AI visibility reporting fails in two directions: a single blended score that nobody can act on, and a raw prompt list that nobody will read. The workable middle is three numbers per platform, one competitive comparison and one sentence about what changed.

How do I report AI visibility to a CEO?

Three numbers and one sentence. Report mention rate, citation rate and absorption rate per platform, name the two competitors you are compared against, and state the single change you made since the last report. A CEO needs to know whether the brand is being recommended and whether that is moving, not how many prompts were run.

Which KPI belongs on a dashboard?

Absorption rate first, because it is the only metric that measures influence. Citation rate second, because it shows whether pages are treated as evidence. Mention rate third, as reach. Share of voice against named competitors is the context line. Keep a blended score if you must, but never as the headline, because it hides which of the three moved.

How do I prove GEO ROI?

Attribution to revenue is weak and should be stated as weak. What can be shown is the chain: citations landing on pages you control, branded search volume, and AI referral traffic where the platform exposes it. Report those three and avoid claiming pipeline that the measurement cannot support, because an overstated case collapses at the first audit.

How do I explain week-to-week volatility?

Show the range, not the point. A prompt that flips between two brands across four runs has a stable answer of 'unsettled', and reporting the average of those flips as a trend is the error. Plot monthly aggregates with the weekly spread behind them, and say plainly that single runs are samples.

What is a realistic first-quarter target?

Technical and entity fixes done, a frozen fifty-prompt baseline established, and movement in at least one platform on a defined cluster. Setting a numeric score target in quarter one invites gaming the prompt library, which is the fastest way to produce a number that improves while nothing else does.

Should GEO sit under SEO, content or PR?

Under whichever team controls the pages that get cited, which is usually SEO or content. PR matters for the third-party corroboration layer but rarely owns the specification pages that win technical questions. The practical test is who can change a product page this week; that is where the function belongs.

How do I report competitor displacement?

Report it per prompt cluster rather than in aggregate. A single line saying you gained four points of share of voice hides that you lost every implementation question while winning the comparison ones. Cluster-level reporting is what turns a competitive number into a content brief.
