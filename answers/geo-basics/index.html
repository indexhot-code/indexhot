GEO Basics: What Generative Engine Optimization Is and How It Works

9 questions · answered directly

Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the work of making a brand appear, get cited and get quoted inside AI-generated answers. It differs from SEO in what it optimises for: not a ranking position, but presence in a synthesised answer and reuse of your specific claims. Everything below answers one of the questions teams ask in their first month.

What is GEO (generative engine optimization)?

GEO is the practice of improving how a brand appears in answers produced by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. It covers three separate outcomes: being named in the answer, being attributed as a source, and having your specific claims or framing reused. The term comes from the 2023 arXiv paper that first measured visibility inside generative answers. Practitioners usually treat citation and absorption as different goals, because a brand can achieve the first without the second.

How is GEO different from SEO?

SEO optimises for a position in a ranked list of links; GEO optimises for presence and wording inside a synthesised answer. The mechanisms overlap — crawlability, indexability and clear structure help both — but the outputs differ. A page can rank first and contribute nothing to an AI answer, and a page outside the top ten can supply the sentence an answer uses. GEO therefore measures mention rate, citation rate and absorption, not rank alone.

How is GEO different from AEO?

AEO, answer engine optimization, usually refers to winning featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes and voice answers. GEO is broader: it covers full generative answers that synthesise several sources, which is where most of the consumption has moved. In practice the tactics overlap heavily — direct answers early in a section serve both — but AEO targets a single extracted passage, while GEO targets an answer assembled from several passages and sources.

Does GEO actually work, or is it a rebrand of SEO?

It works as a distinct discipline where the answer is synthesised rather than listed. The mechanism is documentable: engines retrieve passages, compress them and attach sources, and pages structured to survive that process are used more often. It is not a rebrand, because the levers are different — entity clarity, passage self-containment and citation-source diversity matter far more than keyword placement. It is also not a guaranteed channel: the same content can be ignored on one platform and dominant on another.

How long does GEO take to show results?

Technical and entity fixes usually show up in four to eight weeks, because retrieval picks up new consistency quickly. Content restructuring takes eight to twelve weeks to appear consistently, and third-party corrections take two to four months. Expecting movement inside two weeks leads teams to abandon changes before they have had a chance to be retrieved, which is the most common reason for flat measurements.

Do I need a GEO tool to start?

No. You can run a usable baseline with a browser and a spreadsheet: fifty prompts, four engines, recorded mentions, citations and a note on whether your wording appeared. Tools help with repetition, versioning and coding at scale, but the measurement discipline matters more than the tooling. A bad prompt library measured by an expensive tool is still a bad measurement.

Which AI platforms should I track first?

Start with the platform your buyers actually use, then add the one with the most contrast. ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews carry the largest reach; Perplexity produces the highest citation density and is the easiest place to see whether extraction works. Tracking all four from day one is cheap, but prioritising one for content changes makes it possible to attribute the effect.

Is GEO only for B2B?

No, but the pattern differs. B2B questions tend to be long, comparison-heavy and answered with citations, which makes absorption measurable. Consumer questions are shorter, more often answered without sources, and more sensitive to brand memory. Both benefit from entity clarity and structured facts; B2B benefits more from comparison pages, consumer brands more from corroboration across retail and review sources.

Does GEO work for local businesses?

Partly, and the pattern differs from national brands. Local questions are usually answered with map results and directories rather than a synthesised answer, so the retriever often cites the directory layer instead of your site. The workable route is to make the facts that directories carry — hours, service area, services offered, price range — consistent everywhere, then publish a page that states each of them plainly so a retrieval step has an owned source.
