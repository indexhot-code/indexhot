Measuring AI Visibility: Direct Answers

9 questions · answered directly

AI visibility is measurable, but only if the question set is fixed and the three outcomes are recorded separately. Mention, citation and absorption move independently, and a single score that blends them cannot tell you which one to fix.

How do I measure my brand's AI visibility?

Build a fixed library of prompts, run them on each platform you care about, and record three things per prompt: whether you were named, whether your domain was cited, and whether a distinctive claim of yours appeared. Repeat on a fixed schedule with the same phrasing and log platform and date. That produces a reproducible baseline; anything less produces an anecdote.

How many prompts do I need?

Fifty is a practical default: ten branded and forty non-branded, covering problem, comparison, budget, implementation and use-case intents. Below thirty, single-prompt noise looks like a trend. Above a hundred, marginal precision improves but the time cost usually defeats the discipline of re-running monthly.

Which metrics actually matter?

Three, in this order: absorption rate first, because it measures influence; citation rate second, because it measures whether your pages are treated as evidence; mention rate third, because reach without the other two rarely converts. Share of voice against named competitors is useful context, and any single blended score hides the distinction that matters.

How often should I re-run measurements?

Monthly for trend, weekly only during an active change. Daily measurement produces noise that invites premature conclusions, and quarterly measurement misses the effect of work you have already paid for. Whatever the cadence, keep the prompt set frozen and record every platform and model version.

Can I compare against competitors fairly?

Only within the same prompt set, the same run, the same platform state. Competitor comparisons across different prompt libraries are marketing, not measurement. Run your prompts, include the four to six brands buyers actually mention, and code all of them in one pass so the comparison shares a baseline.

Why do my numbers change every week?

Because retrieval is non-deterministic, models update, and sources compete. A single prompt can produce different sources on consecutive days with no change on your side. The stable signal lives in the aggregate over weeks, which is why the recommendation is a frozen library and monthly aggregation rather than daily monitoring.

What is a good IndexHot score?

On our 0 to 100 scale, 0 to 25 is invisible, 26 to 55 is mentioned, 56 to 80 is cited, and 81 to 100 is recommended. A score in the cited band with low absorption means you are used as evidence but not as argument, which is a content problem rather than a distribution problem.

Can I measure without a tool?

Yes, for up to about twenty prompts a month. A spreadsheet with columns for prompt, platform, mention, citation, absorption level and notes is sufficient, and it forces the coding discipline that tools often hide. Tools earn their cost at scale and when you need version history across months.

What is the difference between a mention and a citation?

A mention is the brand name appearing in the answer text. A citation is the answer attributing a source, usually with a link or a named reference. You can be mentioned in every answer and cited in none, which happens when the model knows the brand but treats no page of yours as evidence. The two are measured separately because they point to different fixes.
