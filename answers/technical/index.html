Technical Access for AI Search: Direct Answers

8 questions · answered directly

Technical failures are the cheapest to fix and the most expensive to ignore, because they cap every other metric at zero. A page that cannot be fetched cannot be cited, and most sites we audit block at least one AI crawler without knowing it.

Do AI crawlers respect robots.txt?

Most named AI crawlers do, which is why an accidental block is common and invisible. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google publish their user-agent strings, and retrieval crawlers generally honour the directives. The complication is that some systems retrieve on behalf of a user rather than crawl on their own account, so a block aimed at one bot can affect a differently named fetcher.

Which AI crawlers should I allow?

Allow the retrieval crawlers for the search products you want to appear in, and decide separately about training crawlers. Retrieval access is what produces citations; training access influences what the model remembers. Many publishers grant the first and restrict the second, which is a defensible position, provided the decision is deliberate rather than an unexamined default.

Does llms.txt work?

It is a proposed convention rather than an adopted standard, and no major engine has confirmed using it for retrieval. It costs little to publish a clean summary file, but it should not be treated as a substitute for crawlability, structured content and indexability. Teams that publish llms.txt and skip the fundamentals see no change.

Does JavaScript block AI crawlers?

Often, yes. Many retrieval fetchers do not execute JavaScript, so content rendered client-side is invisible to them even when Google indexes it. Server-side rendering, static generation or pre-rendered HTML for the key claims is the reliable fix. Checking with a plain HTTP client that runs no scripts is the fastest way to see what a crawler actually gets.

Do I need a CDN rule for AI bots?

Usually something needs changing, because bot protection frequently challenges or blocks non-browser user agents by default. Whitelisting verified AI crawlers, or serving them cached HTML, is often the difference between zero and full citation coverage. This is the single most common silent failure we find in audits.

Does page speed affect AI citations?

Indirectly. Retrieval has time budgets, and a page that times out is a page that cannot be used. Very slow pages are therefore less likely to be retrieved, but the effect is threshold-based rather than a smooth gradient: a page at two seconds is not penalised against one at one second the way a ranking system might treat it.

Do internal links affect AI retrieval?

Yes, in the sense that internal links decide which pages are discovered, prioritised and treated as important. A page with one inbound internal link is rarely the page an engine reaches first. Internal linking is therefore a distribution tool for retrieval, not just a crawl tool, and it is one of the cheapest levers available.

Does an XML sitemap matter for AI crawlers?

Sitemaps are mainly a discovery aid for search engines and a weak signal for retrieval crawlers, which usually fetch pages they already know about. Their practical value is making sure new pages are found quickly by Google, which matters because eligibility in ordinary results gates AI Overviews. They do not compensate for a page that has no internal links.
