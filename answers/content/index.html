Content for AI Answers: Direct Answers

8 questions · answered directly

Content that gets cited is not necessarily longer; it is self-contained. The unit that matters is the passage, not the page: a section that states one complete claim, with its conditions, can be lifted intact. Everything below is about building passages that survive that lift.

How long should a page be to get cited?

Long enough to cover the question completely, which is usually 600 to 1,200 words for an informational page and 400 to 800 for a definition. Length by itself moves nothing; structure does. A 400-word page that answers one question in its first sentence outperforms a 2,000-word page that answers it across six sections.

Do FAQ sections help with AI citations?

Often, when the questions are real and the answers are specific. An FAQ block gives retrieval a set of self-contained question-answer pairs, which is exactly the shape a summary needs. The common failure is generic questions with marketing answers, which adds length and no extractable content.

Should I write for AI or for people?

For people, structured for extraction. The two are not in conflict: a first-sentence answer serves a reader in a hurry and a retrieval step equally. Writing that reads as machine-targeted — keyword repetition, listicle padding, unnatural phrasing — usually reduces both reader trust and extractability.

Do lists and tables get cited more than prose?

Tables and lists are more often lifted verbatim, because they carry facts without needing surrounding argument. Specifications, prices, dimensions and comparisons are routinely reproduced almost exactly. Prose gets cited when it states a claim cleanly in one sentence; narrative that builds to a conclusion is rarely used at all.

Does content freshness matter?

For time-sensitive facts it matters more than depth. Undated pages lose to dated competitors on pricing, policy, availability and compatibility questions. Adding a revision date near the fact, and actually updating the fact, is one of the highest-return changes available, and it costs almost nothing.

Should I publish one long page or several short ones?

One page per question cluster, not per keyword. Several near-duplicate pages split the candidate signal and can result in none being used while a competitor's single page wins. Consolidation into one well-structured page usually produces a measurable change within weeks.

Do I need different content for each platform?

Not different content, but different emphasis. Perplexity rewards freshness and per-subquery completeness; ChatGPT rewards specific checkable claims that survive compression; AI Overviews rewards clean opening sentences and eligibility. One well-structured page can serve all three, which is why the recommendation is structure first, platform second.

Should I delete pages that never get cited?

Not by default. A page can be uncited and still earn its place by supporting a cited page through internal links or by answering a question no engine currently asks. Delete when the page competes with a near-duplicate and splits the candidate signal, which is the case where consolidation measurably helps, or when it is genuinely thin and adds no distinct claim.
