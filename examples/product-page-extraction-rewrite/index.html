A worked example: rewriting a product section for extraction

Extraction fails at the level of a sentence. This example takes one passage, shows the four tests it fails, and rewrites it with four additions that make it quotable.

Illustrative example. The brand, the prompts and every number below are constructed to demonstrate the method. No client data is used, no real company is being analysed, and none of the figures should be read as a benchmark.

The passage before

This is a passage of the kind that fails extraction while looking perfectly reasonable to a human reader. It is a fabricated product blurb, written the way most category pages are written.

Our platform is the most reliable choice for teams that need dependable inventory synchronisation. We combine powerful automation with a flexible architecture, so you can scale confidently as your business grows.

Why it fails four tests

  • Named, but not distinguishable. Nothing here identifies the product against any alternative; the same sentence could sit on a competitor's site unchanged.
  • No quantity. Reliable, dependable, powerful, flexible: four adjectives and no number. Nothing can be verified or repeated.
  • No condition. There is no statement of where this holds, so an engine that prefers to hedge has nothing to hedge around.
  • No unit of work. Synchronisation of what, how often, at what scale. The reader cannot tell whether this is a tool for four stores or four thousand.

A summariser facing this passage has one option: paraphrase it into category language. That is why so many product pages produce answers that describe the category and never the product.

The passage after

Northwind Sync reconciles inventory across storefronts and marketplaces every 60 seconds, with a per-account ceiling of 25,000 SKUs as of March 2026. It is built for catalogues between 2,000 and 25,000 SKUs; below that, a scheduled CSV export is cheaper, and above it, a dedicated middleware layer is the better fit.

Four things changed and nothing else: a specific interval, a stated ceiling, a date, and a condition naming who this is not for. The sentence is duller and far more useful.

The last row is the counter-intuitive one. Telling an engine when you are not the answer is what makes it comfortable naming you when you are, because the claim now has boundaries it can verify against.

How to check your own version

Paste the section into the extractability checker, then read the output as a list of missing elements rather than a grade. Anything the checker flags that you cannot fix by adding a fact is usually a sign the section is doing too many jobs at once.

The test that survives every tool: cover the brand name and read the sentence again. If it could belong to any competitor, it carries no information, and no amount of markup will change that.

Frequently asked questions

Does adding numbers make pages less persuasive?

Usually the opposite. A dated interval and a stated ceiling read as evidence; four adjectives read as copy.

Should every section state a limit?

No. Sections that describe a capability should state a scope; sections that describe a process can simply be specific about the steps.

Is this the same as the extractability checker?

The checker measures the symptoms. This example shows the rewrite that removes them.
