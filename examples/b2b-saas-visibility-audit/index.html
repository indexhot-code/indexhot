A worked example: a B2B SaaS visibility audit

One brand, fifty prompts, two runs. This example shows the sequence we recommend: measure both halves, find the cluster that fails, change structure rather than volume, then re-run and compare the same columns.

Illustrative example. The brand, the prompts and every number below are constructed to demonstrate the method. No client data is used, no real company is being analysed, and none of the figures should be read as a benchmark.

The setup

A fabricated B2B inventory-sync product, Northwind Sync, is measured with the standard library: fifty prompts, ten branded and forty unbranded, run across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini in one pass, with AI Overviews recorded separately.

Before any content change, every answer is coded for the three outcomes. The point of the first run is not to feel good about a number; it is to locate where the loss happens, because the fix differs by location.

Read down the columns rather than across the rows. Branded recall is almost complete and absorption is almost zero, which is a specific condition: the engines can describe Northwind and will not adopt its framing. Unbranded recall is weakest exactly where buyers do not yet know a category exists, which is where new demand would come from.

Where the loss was concentrated

Ten of the forty unbranded prompts resolve to integration documentation, and eight of those name a competitor. Northwind's own documentation is excellent and client-side rendered, so the passages that would answer the question cannot be retrieved at all.

Absorption fails for a duller reason: the pages make comparative claims with no quantities. Nothing in the copy can be lifted and repeated, so the engine paraphrases around it and the answer ends up describing the category rather than the product.

  • Documentation behind a renderer: invisible to retrieval, however good it is.
  • Comparative claims without numbers: unquotable, so paraphrased away.
  • Pricing pages that list tiers without stating what changes between them.

What we changed

  1. Serve the integration docs as static HTML, one question per page, with the answer in the first sentence.
  2. Add quantities to every comparative claim: seat thresholds, sync intervals, record limits, and the month those numbers were measured.
  3. Publish one problem-first page per failure mode, written for a buyer who does not know the category term yet.

Three changes, all of them structural. No new blog programme, no keyword rewrite, and nothing added that could not be traced to a specific failing prompt cluster.

The second run

Constructed to show the shape of the change rather than a result: unbranded naming rises from nineteen to twenty-eight of forty, citations from ten to nineteen, and absorption from two to nine. Branded numbers barely move, which is the expected pattern when the work is aimed at the unbranded half.

The absorption gain is the one worth watching. It moves last and it moves least, because it depends on the engine choosing to repeat a specific claim, which is a decision made about your page and not about your category.

What this example can and cannot prove

It can show the sequence: measure both halves, locate the failure by cluster, fix structure rather than volume, re-run and compare. That sequence is the standard's whole argument.

It cannot tell you what your numbers will be. Sample sizes here are illustrative, the prompts are invented, and a single run of an invented brand cannot establish a trend. Use it to check your own report for the same three columns, not to benchmark against.

Frequently asked questions

Is Northwind Sync a real company?

No. It is invented for this page so that the method can be shown without disclosing client work or making claims about a real brand.

Could the numbers be different for my category?

Almost certainly. Cluster shares differ by category; the method of locating failure by cluster does not.

Why does absorption move last?

Because it depends on an engine adopting a specific claim, which requires the claim to be quotable and corroborated. Naming and citing can improve before that happens.
