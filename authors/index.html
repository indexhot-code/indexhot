Who writes IndexHot

Last updated November 26, 2026

Every page on this site is written and checked by the same small team, and bylines are organisational rather than personal because attribution we cannot verify is worse than none. What follows is what we can be held to: the roles, the checking process, and how corrections happen.

Three roles, one standard

The work divides into measurement, editing and engineering. A page is only published when all three have signed off on what it claims.

  • Measurement. Owns the question library, the coding rubric and the scoring arithmetic. Anything that states a rate or a score comes from this role, and the sample size travels with the number.
  • Editing. Owns structure, definitions and comparability across pages. This role enforces the rule that a claim appearing on ten pages is worded identically in all ten.
  • Engineering. Owns access, rendering and the validity of the published artefacts: sitemap, status codes, schema and the released zip.

No single role can publish alone. That constraint is deliberate: it catches the two failure modes this field produces most, which are a number with no stated sample and a page that is correct but unreachable.

How a claim gets checked

Four rules apply to every factual statement outside the definitions.

  1. External claims cite the source. A study, a platform behaviour or a market figure links to where it came from, and the link is to the original rather than to a summary of a summary.
  2. Own figures state the sample. Any rate we report carries the number of prompts, brands, platforms and dates it was computed from. A percentage without a denominator does not get published.
  3. Limits are written into the page. Where a category has fewer than forty relevant answers in our sample, the page says so rather than implying precision.
  4. Two people code before a rubric ships. Absorption judgements are calibrated on twenty answers by two coders; disagreements are resolved by adding an example to the rubric, not by argument.

What we do not do

  • We do not publish invented case studies. Where a page needs an example, it uses public data or our own sample, and says which.
  • We do not take payment for placement, review text or comparison framing. No vendor sees a comparison page before it is live.
  • We do not promise rankings, mentions or citations. Visibility is a measured outcome, not a deliverable we can guarantee.

Corrections

Errors are corrected in the page, with the update date changed and the correction stated where it changes a conclusion. We do not run a separate changelog, because a correction that lives elsewhere does not reach the reader who saw the wrong figure.

If a page makes a claim about a vendor and that vendor's positioning changes, the page is revised rather than annotated. The comparison pages carry a dated disclaimer for exactly this reason.

Contact

Corrections, methodological objections and citation requests all go to hello@indexhot.com. Objections with a source get answered first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I cite an IndexHot page?

Yes. Measurement definitions come from the published standard, which carries a stable citation. Attribute figures to the specific page they appear on.

Do you publish sponsored content?

No. No page on this site is paid placement, and no vendor has reviewed a comparison page before publication.

How do I report an error?

Email hello@indexhot.com with the URL and the claim you believe is wrong. Factual errors are corrected in the page, with the date changed.
