The Absorption Playbook: How to Write Claims That Get Repeated

By IndexHot Team · November 28, 2026 · 12 sections

Citation gets you listed. Absorption decides whether anything you wrote survives into the answer, and therefore whether anyone reading it learns what you claim. This playbook covers the four levels, the five attributes that make a claim repeatable, the five tests that catch an unquotable sentence, and a rewrite sequence for the page that is already cited and contributes nothing.

Why absorption decides influence

A citation is an address. Absorption is whether anything from that address entered the answer. A brand cited in twelve answers and repeated in none has bought visibility without influence: it appears in a source list and contributes no sentence that a buyer would remember.

This is why the standard weights absorption above mention and citation when the three are combined into a score. Naming is cheap and citations are partly a function of how many sources a surface attaches. Repetition of a specific claim is the only outcome that requires the model to have read, trusted and chosen your sentence.

One-sentence version: if a stranger reading the answer could not tell what you claim, you were cited and not absorbed. That is a different problem from being absent, and it needs a different fix.

The four levels, with examples

Absorption is coded at four levels, and the code is taken from the strongest instance in the answer rather than averaged across sentences.

LevelWhat it meansExample
L0
No reuse at all
You appear in a source list; the answer describes the category

L2 is the workhorse level and the one most brands never reach. It does not require being recommended. It requires one verifiable thing you said to appear in the answer, attributable to you.

L3 is rarer and it depends on L2: an answer recommends what it can justify, and it justifies with facts. Skipping L2 to chase L3 produces copy that asserts a position with nothing behind it, which models decline to repeat.

What makes a claim repeatable

Five attributes recur across every quoted claim we have coded. They are not stylistic preferences; each one removes a reason for a model to paraphrase instead of repeat.

  • A unit. How many, how long, how much, at what interval. A claim with no unit is a mood.
  • A boundary. Where it holds and where it stops holding. Boundaries are what allow a hedged answer to name you.
  • A date. When it was true. Undated facts are unsafe to repeat on volatile topics.
  • A method. Enough about how the number was produced that it is not a bare assertion.
  • A subject. The sentence names the thing it is about, rather than pointing back at a paragraph.

Claims with all five get repeated. Claims with three get summarised. Claims with one get dropped. The distribution is not subtle, and it is visible in a corpus of answers if you code for it.

Five tests you can run in a minute

  1. Cover-name. Delete your brand. If the sentence still reads as a claim about the category and nobody could tell it was yours, it carries no attribution value.
  2. Standalone. Paste the sentence into an empty document. If it needs the previous sentence to make sense, it will not survive extraction.
  3. Check. Ask what would falsify it. If nothing could, it is not a fact.
  4. Negative. State when you are the wrong choice. A page that never says so has no boundary for an answer to hedge around.
  5. Translation. Restate it in one plain sentence for a colleague outside your function. If that requires a paragraph of preamble, the claim is buried.

Run these on the three sentences you most want quoted, not on the whole page. The page is not the unit that gets selected; the sentence is.

Note the direction of the failures: in each case the page supplied something a human would accept and a machine would not. The problem is not credibility for a reader; it is liftability for a system that must reproduce a sentence without changing its meaning.

Rewriting at three levels

Sentence level

Make the sentence carry its own subject, unit and date. Not we are one of the fastest, but reconciliation runs every 60 seconds with a 25,000 SKU ceiling as of March 2026. The second version can be copied; the first cannot.

Section level

Open with the claim, then spend the rest of the section on the conditions under which it holds. Sections that build to a conclusion produce a chunk whose meaning lives in the last sentence, which is the sentence most likely to be cut.

Page level

Give each section one question. A page answering six questions produces six chunks that compete with each other, and the competition is resolved by whichever chunk is most self-contained, not by whichever you consider most important.

Absorption is not persuasion

The incentive here is to write claims that are easy to repeat, and that incentive can produce confident nonsense. A specific, dated, bounded claim that happens to be wrong will be repeated, and then contradicted by a competitor's better-sourced page, and the second correction travels faster than the first claim.

The rule we apply: optimise for repeatability only among claims you can defend with a source and a date. Repeatability is a distribution property; truth is a precondition, not a variable.

A claim with a method and a date can be checked by a reader. That is also what makes it safe to repeat. The two properties are the same property, which is why this discipline does not reward fabrication for long.

Absorption differs by surface

The same page can be fully absorbed on one surface and merely cited on another, because the surfaces compress differently. Coding absorption per surface rather than as a blended rate is therefore not pedantry; it is the difference between a finding and a statistic.

SurfaceTypical compressionWhat survives
ChatGPT
Few sources, long answer, framing often taken from one page
Framing and a specific claim can both survive

The practical consequence: an absorption programme that optimises one surface will look like it is failing on another. On Perplexity the target is one quotable figure per section. On AI Overviews it is one figure per page. On Claude it is a stated condition. Chasing L3 on a surface that compresses to two sentences is a category error.

Measuring it

Absorption is coded per answer, per surface, from the strongest instance, by two people on a calibration set of twenty before either codes the batch. The rubric is published so that a disagreement can be located at a specific level rather than argued in general.

  • Code the level, not a score out of ten; scores invite averaging across incomparable cases.
  • Record the sentence that was reused, because the pattern across answers is the finding.
  • Do not average absorption across surfaces with different citation behaviour; report it per surface.
  • Keep a sample of L0 answers where you were cited, since those are the most instructive failures.

A quarterly absorption rate that moves by ten points is a real change; one that moves by two is inside the noise of a fifty-prompt sample.

The three objections, answered

We cannot publish numbers

You almost certainly can publish one: a response time, a threshold, a supported range, a tenure, a count of something you actually know. If absolutely nothing measurable exists, that is a finding about the product's positioning rather than a constraint on the writing, and it is worth saying so internally.

Legal will not approve specifics

Legal objects to unqualified promises, not to qualified facts. A sentence that states a limit and a date is usually easier to approve than a sentence that claims superiority, because the first is defensible and the second is not.

Specifics make us look small

A stated scope reads as confidence to a buyer who has a scope problem, and it reads as trustworthiness to a system deciding whether to repeat you. The alternative, a page that claims to suit everyone, is the one that gets summarised into the category and never into the brand.

A worked sequence on one page

  1. Pick the page that already receives citations and zero absorption. That is the cheapest possible candidate.
  2. Find the three sentences you most want quoted, and run the five tests on each.
  3. Rewrite each so it carries a subject, a unit, a boundary and a date, without adding a paragraph.
  4. Publish the negative case as its own short section, adjacent to the claim.
  5. Wait a full measurement cycle before judging; absorption moves last, after mention and citation.

One page, three sentences, one cycle. In our experience that is a better use of a week than four new articles, because it attacks the only outcome that changes what a buyer believes.

Frequently asked questions

Is absorption the same as being recommended?

No. Recommendation is the strongest level, but claim-level reuse is the level most brands should aim for first, and it is reachable without being the top pick.

Can I raise absorption without new content?

Usually yes. Rewriting three sentences on a page that already gets cited is the highest-return change available, and it costs an afternoon.

Does absorption apply to AI Overviews?

It applies, with a caveat: overviews compress harder, so claim-level reuse is rarer there and a single precise figure is worth more than a paragraph of context.
