Prompt Sets for Non-English Markets Are Not Translations

By IndexHot Team · November 28, 2026

If your library is English and your market is not, the measurement is of your translation rather than of your position. Buyers in other languages ask with different phrasing, different category words, and sometimes different questions entirely.

Why translation fails as a method

A translated prompt imports English assumptions: the category name, the comparison set, the unit of purchase. In markets where the category is known by a local term, or where buyers search by regulation rather than by product, the translated prompt returns an answer about a market that does not exist.

The failure is invisible in the report. Rates look plausible and comparable, which is worse than looking broken, because it invites cross-market conclusions that the data cannot support.

What a locale-specific set contains

  • The category term as buyers actually use it, including the local loanword if one dominates.
  • Comparison prompts naming the competitors that are actually present in that market, not your global set.
  • Regulatory or standards prompts, which frequently outrank product prompts in regulated categories.
  • The purchase unit: per seat, per container, per square metre, per month, as locally understood.
  • One prompt in the local language for a mixed-language query, since real queries mix.

Three ways multilingual measurement goes wrong

MistakeWhat it produces
Blending locales into one rate
A number describing the mix of markets sampled, not any market

Implementation, cheaply

  1. Run one locale properly rather than five badly. One market, twenty prompts, both halves, three runs.
  2. Have a native speaker review the prompts, not the answers. Prompt wording is where the market assumption lives.
  3. Publish local-language pages for the terms you measure, or the measurement will describe someone else's pages.
  4. Report locale separately in every table, and never in a blended column.

If you cannot review prompts with someone who buys in that language, do not publish the number. An unreviewed translated set is a claim about your translator wearing the costume of a market study.

The entity side

Local-language pages need their own entity signals: the brand name as written locally, the local legal entity if one exists, sameAs links between the language versions, and hreflang so the language versions are understood as alternates rather than duplicates. Without those, a model may treat the local site as an unrelated company with a similar name, which is the worst of both outcomes.
