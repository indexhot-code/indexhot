Google AI Overviews: The Two Gates Your Page Has to Pass

By IndexHot Team · November 9, 2026

AI Overviews has two gates, and most teams optimise for the wrong one. A page must first be eligible in ordinary Google results, then it must be extractable enough for a summary to use without losing accuracy. Rewriting content before fixing eligibility is work spent on a page that cannot appear at all.

Gate one: eligibility

AI Overviews draws from pages Google already considers eligible for the query. In most cases that means a ranking in the top ten, though not every top-ten result is used and not every query produces an overview.

The consequences are dull but decisive. A page excluded from the index, canonicalised to a different URL, or stuck behind a noindex tag cannot appear in the summary no matter how well it is written. So can a page that ranks on page two, because it is not in the candidate pool.

The first diagnostic question is therefore never 'how do I improve the content' but 'is this page eligible for this query at all'. Checking index status, canonical target, robots directives and average position takes an hour and prevents weeks of wasted editing.

Gate two: extractability

Once eligible, a page still has to survive compression. The summary is short, so it uses passages that state a complete point without requiring the surrounding argument. Pages that build to a conclusion over several paragraphs rarely contribute anything beyond a generic sentence.

Three habits raise survival rates. Put the answer in the first sentence of a section, then explain. Keep conditions and figures in the same sentence, because a summary that drops the condition becomes wrong and the model avoids it. Publish tables and specifications as text, since data inside an image cannot be extracted.

The most common single failure we see is a page that opens with three hundred words of context before defining anything. That page may rank well and still never appear in an overview, because there is no clean passage to lift.

What we see failing most

Ranking without extraction is the dominant pattern: pages in the top five that are excluded from the overview because their key claim is spread across sections. These pages need restructuring, not rewriting.

The second pattern is extraction without eligibility: beautifully structured pages that sit on page three, often because they target a phrasing nobody uses or because internal links never prioritised them. These need distribution work, not more content.

The third, less obvious pattern is competing with yourself. Two near-duplicate pages on the same topic split the candidate signal, and in some cases neither is used while a competitor's single page is. Consolidation often produces an immediate change.

Fix order

Step one: confirm eligibility for the target query. Check index status, canonical, robots, and current position. If position is below the first page, fix internal links and consolidation before touching the copy.

Step two: restructure the opening. Move the definition into the first sentence, and make sure the first paragraph answers the question without depending on what follows.

Step three: convert embedded data. Any table, specification or price that exists as an image or a PDF becomes text, with units and a visible date.

Step four: consolidate duplicates, then re-check. Two pages merging into one usually outperforms two pages competing.

How to measure inclusion

Measure inclusion separately from ranking. A page can hold a top-five rank and appear in the overview for one phrasing and not another, so a single check overstates either success or failure.

Run the same query in several phrasings, record whether an overview appeared, whether you were named, and whether a link was attached. Repeat monthly. The useful signal is the proportion of phrasings where you appear, not any single result.

If you are named without a link, you have a mention and no citation, which is a different problem from being absent: the answer knows you exist but does not treat your page as evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to rank number one to appear in AI Overviews?

Usually not, but you need to be in the candidate pool, which in practice means the first page of results. Position within that pool influences selection, but extractability decides whether a specific passage is used.

Why did my page appear in an overview once and then stop?

Inclusion is unstable across phrasings, regions and model updates. It can also change when a competitor publishes a cleaner version of the same claim. Repeated measurement across several phrasings is the only way to tell noise from a real change.

Can AI Overviews mention my brand without linking my site?

Yes. A summary can reference a brand or product without attaching a visible source link. Mention and citation must therefore be measured separately, since the first can happen without the second.
