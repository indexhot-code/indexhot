GEO with no budget: six fixes that cost only time

By IndexHot Team · November 26, 2026 · 8 min read

Most of what moves AI visibility is not paid for. It is structural work on pages you already have, done in the right order, plus a small measurement habit. These six fixes are available to a team with no tooling budget, and together they cover the failures that account for most absence.

Fix the plumbing before the prose

Two technical conditions decide whether anything else can work: the page must return a real status code and the content must be readable without scripts.

Unknown paths returning 200 with a template is the classic soft 404, and it teaches crawlers to distrust the site. On a small site this is fixable in an afternoon: test four URL shapes and correct what is wrong.

  • Unknown path returns 404 with a page that says so.
  • Bare directory returns 301 to the trailing-slash version.
  • Retired page returns 410 or 301, never 200 with a placeholder.

Then fetch your ten most important pages as raw HTML. If the substance is not there, no rewrite reaches the answer.

Say what you are, once, identically

Ambiguity is free to fix and expensive to ignore. Write one sentence stating category, location and product, then use it unchanged on the homepage, the about page, the schema and every external profile you control.

The same form matters. Three paraphrases of the same claim read as three claims, which is why teams that describe themselves differently everywhere are described inconsistently by models.

Restructure existing pages into question sections

Pick the ten questions your sales team hears most and give each one a heading on the page that already covers the topic. This is editing, not writing, and it usually doubles the number of usable chunks.

Each section should open with a direct answer in one sentence, then support it. Two hundred words that answer a question beat two thousand that discuss a subject, because the chunk that survives cutting is the answer.

Build a fifty-prompt library in a spreadsheet

Measurement without tools is possible. Ten branded prompts, forty non-branded across eight intent clusters, three runs each, coded into three columns: mentioned, cited, absorbed.

The whole cycle takes about three hours and produces the only number that tells you whether the rest of the work is succeeding. Without it, every change is a guess followed by another guess.

Add internal links that carry meaning

Crawlers and retrieval systems both use link context. A link whose anchor text says what it points to is worth more than a link labelled read more, and it is free.

  • Link from hub pages to every child page, with descriptive anchors.
  • Link across clusters where a term appears, so the entity graph is connected.
  • Keep the number of links per page sane; forty links dilute the signal each one carries.

Date what changes and stop dating what does not

Freshness matters where facts are volatile: prices, limits, schedules, versions. It does not matter for definitions, and stamping a new date on unchanged content is detected as unreliable rather than fresh.

Show the date on the page, not only in the sitemap, because extraction reads the page. And when you revise a figure, revise the figure, not just the timestamp.

Frequently asked questions

What should a no-budget team do first?

Fix status codes and script dependency. Both are binary, both are testable in an hour, and both invalidate other work when they fail.

Do I need a paid tool to measure?

No. A spreadsheet with fifty prompts and three coded columns is enough for a defensible baseline.

Is restructuring pages enough without new content?

Often for mention and citation, rarely for absorption. Absorption needs specificity, which sometimes means new facts rather than new pages.
