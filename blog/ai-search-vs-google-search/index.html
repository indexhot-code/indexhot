AI search is not Google search: four differences that change the work

By IndexHot Team · November 26, 2026 · 9 min read

The instinct to treat AI answers as a new ranking surface is understandable and mostly wrong. Four differences do the real work: what counts as entry, what unit is evaluated, how attribution behaves, and how stable the result is. Understanding them explains why some well-ranked pages are invisible and why some low-authority pages get quoted constantly.

Entry is not a position

In a results page, entry means occupying a rank. In an answer, entry means being inside the set of entities the response discusses, and that set is usually three to six names.

Position within the set exists but is soft: first in a list, subject of a paragraph, parenthetical at the end. The bigger distinction is membership itself, and there is no page-one equivalent that guarantees it.

This changes the question from how do I rank to am I in the option space, and the two require different work. Ranking work optimises a URL against a query; membership work establishes an entity that retrieval will treat as relevant.

The unit is a passage, not a page

Search engines evaluate documents. Answer systems retrieve and rank passages, cut to a few hundred tokens, often at heading boundaries. Whatever survives that cut is what gets used.

The consequence is that a page can be excellent and unusable: its argument is coherent across sections and incoherent inside any one of them. A page of self-contained sections is weaker as prose and much stronger as a source.

  • Name the subject at the start of every section rather than relying on pronouns.
  • Put the condition next to the figure, not two paragraphs earlier.
  • Give tables their own labels so a row survives without the surrounding text.

This is also why heading structure matters more than keyword placement. Headings are the cut points and, in many pipelines, the context prepended to a chunk.

Attribution is discretionary

A results page must show links; that is the product. An answer may attach sources, may attach a few, or may attach none, and the decision is a product choice rather than a ranking output.

Where sources are attached, position in the list tends to reflect retrieval order rather than influence. Where few are attached, the one that appears usually supplied the framing. Neither is a reliable proxy for how much of your content was used.

This is why absorption has to be coded separately. A citation proves the pipeline reached you; absorption proves something of yours survived the synthesis. Treating the first as the second is the most common reporting error in this field.

Stability is lower, and the variance is structural

Sampling, index updates and model releases all move answers, and a single run is a sample rather than a result. In a fifty-prompt library, expect a handful of prompts to flip between runs even when nothing changed.

The measurement consequence is a cadence: monthly for trend, weekly only during an active change, three runs per prompt with the median coded. Anything faster produces movements that look like trends and are not.

The strategic consequence is patience on the right things. Recall and absorption move over quarters; mention and citation move in weeks. Teams that expect all four on the same clock either give up early or keep changing things that were working.

What transfers from SEO, and what does not

Transfers: technical access, clean status codes, crawlable internal links, clear entity description, structured data, and the discipline of answering real questions.

Does not transfer cleanly: keyword density targets, backlink volume as a proxy for authority, title-length optimisation, and the assumption that a ranking report describes visibility. Each of those has a partial analogue at best.

The overlap is large enough that SEO teams are well positioned, provided they replace the ranking question with the membership question and the page unit with the passage unit. Those two substitutions account for most of the difference in outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Does ranking in Google help in AI answers?

Somewhat, through eligibility on Google's own AI surface. On other surfaces the connection is weak; retrieval selects on passage relevance, not on your rank.

Should I stop doing SEO?

No. Search still supplies the majority of most sites' traffic, and the technical work overlaps almost entirely.

What is the single biggest mistake?

Reporting citations as influence. Citation means reached; absorption means used.
