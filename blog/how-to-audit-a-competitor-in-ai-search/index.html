How to audit a competitor's AI visibility without their data

By IndexHot Team · November 26, 2026 · 8 min read

You cannot see a competitor's internal reporting, but you can see almost everything that matters: which answers name them, which pages the answers cite, and what those pages contain. That is enough to reconstruct why they win and where the gap is, using only public information.

Step 1: build one prompt set for the category

Use your own library rather than a competitor-specific one, so the comparison is same-run and same-date. Fifty prompts across the eight intent clusters is enough; the point is coverage of the decision, not volume.

Record the answers verbatim. Screenshots lose the text that coding depends on, and the sentences matter more than the layout.

Step 2: code the competitor set, not just the brand

For each answer, note every brand named, in order, and whether each is described or merely listed. The competitor's pattern is the finding: if they appear in seven of eight clusters and are described in five, their strength is breadth plus extractable facts.

  • Which clusters they own outright, and which they are absent from.
  • Whether they are named only, or described with a claim.
  • Whether their presence is stable across three runs or unsettled.

Step 3: read the pages that get cited

This is the most informative step and the one most often skipped. For each answer citing the competitor, open the cited page and look at four things: does the claim appear in the first sentence of a section, is it in text rather than an image, does the section name its subject, and is there a table.

You will usually find that the winning pages are not better written. They are more cuttable: each section states a claim that survives being read alone.

Step 4: compare structure, not quality

Put one of their cited pages beside one of yours and compare the same attributes: heading-as-question, claim in the first sentence, table with labelled columns, date visible, schema present.

This reframes the problem from they write better to their pages are structured for extraction, which is a fixable difference and a cheaper one than a content programme.

Step 5: find the sub-questions nobody covers

The gap is rarely the headline question. It is the follow-ups: migration, limits, support terms, integration specifics, pricing at a particular scale. Those are separate retrievals, and incumbents frequently neglect them because they wrote one comprehensive page years ago.

List five such sub-questions where the competitor is absent. That list is your content brief, and it is more valuable than a share-of-voice figure, because it names work that would change the number.

Frequently asked questions

Is this ethical?

Yes. Every input is a public answer and a public page. Nothing here requires access to their analytics or private data.

How often should I re-run a competitor audit?

Quarterly, on the same library. More often produces drift rather than insight, and the competitor's own content changes slowly.

What if the competitor is cited everywhere and we are absent?

Then step 5 is your whole strategy for a quarter. Entering a closed set through the sub-questions is cheaper than contesting the headline question.
