How We Score Citation Absorption: Method, Rubric, and Failure Modes

By IndexHot Team · November 5, 2026

Citation absorption is the one GEO metric that cannot be automated reliably, because it asks whether an answer adopted your meaning rather than whether it linked your domain. We score it with a four-level rubric applied by two independent coders, and we report the distribution rather than a mean. Without that discipline the number drifts toward whatever the analyst expected to find.

Why a citation is not enough

A citation proves a source was attached. It does not prove the source changed the answer. Engines that attach ten sources per response produce high citation counts regardless of whether any single source contributed a sentence.

Absorption is the metric that distinguishes recognition from influence. An answer that repeats your specific threshold, your framing or your recommendation persuades on your behalf. An answer that lists your domain at the bottom does not.

This distinction is also what makes the metric useful for content decisions: absorption responds to specificity, while citation count responds to structure and access. They need different fixes, so they need to be measured separately.

The four-level rubric

Level 0, none: the domain is attached but nothing from the page appears in the answer beyond the name or a generic category label.

Level 1, topical: the answer uses a topic or theme associated with your page, but the wording could have come from any competitor in the category.

Level 2, claim-level: a specific, checkable claim from your page appears in the answer in substance, such as a figure, a threshold or a defined condition, even if paraphrased.

Level 3, recommendation-level: the answer adopts your positioning or recommendation, for example naming you as the choice for a specific use case, or reproducing your reasoning about trade-offs.

The gap between level 1 and level 2 is where most reporting is inflated. Level 1 feels like success in a coding sheet and represents almost no influence in reality.

How to code an answer

Before scoring, write down five to eight claims from the source page that are distinctive enough that a competitor could not plausibly supply them. These become the anchor list for the whole sample.

Then read the answer with the source page beside it, and mark the highest level reached anywhere in the answer. Do not average across sentences; a single level-3 sentence is a level-3 answer.

Two coders score the same twenty-answer calibration set independently. Where they disagree by more than one level, the rubric gets an example, not an argument. Only after calibration does the bulk of the scoring begin.

Five failure modes

Keyword matching: counting the brand name or a product term as evidence of absorption. Naming you is not using you.

Generic claims: scoring absorption on a statement any competitor could supply, which turns level 2 into a level 1 with better marketing.

Single-coder drift: one analyst scoring a long sample alone, where the standard slowly relaxes as fatigue sets in.

Averaging over incongruent cases: reporting a mean of 1.4 when the sample is half zeros and half threes, which describes no actual answer.

No re-baselining: changing the rubric mid-study and reporting the trend as continuous.

How we report it

We publish absorption rate as the share of prompts reaching level 2 or above, plus the distribution across all four levels. A single mean hides whether the movement came from converting zeros into ones or ones into threes.

We also report absorption per platform, because the engines differ structurally: source-heavy engines compress more and score lower, while source-light engines score higher on the same content.

Finally, we keep the coded answers. A number without the underlying text cannot be audited by anyone else, and an unauditable number is not a measurement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is absorption scoring subjective?

It is judgement-based, which is not the same as arbitrary. A written rubric, a calibration set and two coders make it reproducible within roughly one level, which is enough for trend decisions but not for precise rankings.

Can absorption be automated with an LLM judge?

Partly. A model can flag candidate claim overlap quickly, but it tends to over-reward paraphrase and under-detect negation. We use it for triage and keep human coding for the sample that drives decisions.

What absorption rate should we expect?

On source-heavy engines, single digits are common even for strong brands. On source-light engines, twenty to forty percent is achievable. The number matters only against your own baseline and your competitors'.
