When a competitor owns the answer: four responses that work

By IndexHot Team · November 26, 2026 · 8 min read

Some categories have a clear answer owner: one brand appears in nearly every response, and the gap looks structural rather than tactical. The instinct is to write more and hope. Four responses work better, and the first is to stop competing on the question the incumbent already owns.

First, check whether the set is really closed

A single answer naming one brand is a sample, not a market. Before concluding that a competitor owns the category, run three times per prompt across surfaces and union the results. Concentration is often real, but it is also often an artefact of one query phrasing.

Measure concentration properly: which domains supply half the citations, and how many unique brands appear across the set. Three domains supplying most citations is a genuinely closed category; one brand leading a field of eight is a normal market with an incumbent.

Widen the question set rather than fight on one question

If a competitor owns best CRM for small teams, that phrasing is expensive to contest. The adjacent questions are cheaper: migration from a specific tool, pricing at a specific seat count, hiring an admin, data export limits.

These are the sub-questions fan-out produces, and each is a separate retrieval. Winning four of them produces presence in the clustered answer even when the headline question stays lost.

It also builds the evidence base that eventually contests the headline: a model with four specific pages from you and one generic page from them has more to draw on for the broader question.

Correct facts you can prove, and be specific about it

Incumbents are frequently described wrongly: stale pricing, discontinued features, wrong ownership after an acquisition. Where the error is factual and you can demonstrate it, a dated, explicit correction page is worth writing.

The page must state the correct value, the date, and the basis, and it must be findable from the pages that carry the wrong figure. A correction that lives only in a press release is not retrievable.

  • State the correct value first, in the first sentence, with the date.
  • Link from pricing and product pages so the correction is reachable.
  • Avoid arguing about preference; argue only about verifiable facts.

Build corroboration outside your own domain

Selection favours sources that are corroborated. A claim that appears only on your site is weaker than the same claim appearing in a review platform, a directory, a trade publication and a partner page.

This is the slowest of the four responses and the only durable one. It means maintaining profiles, responding to directory editors, publishing data others can cite, and being present where the category is discussed rather than only where it is sold.

The measurement is straightforward: count how many independent domains state your key facts. If the number is one, you are the only source and the model has thin evidence.

Accept the parts you cannot win

Some answers will keep going to the incumbent for structural reasons: it has a decade of third-party coverage, a knowledge panel and a category-defining product. Spending against that is expensive and slow.

The useful question is which answers actually precede a purchase in your segment. A brand that wins the comparison answer but loses the definition answer may still be fine, because buyers reach comparisons later and with more intent.

Report that honestly. A dashboard showing 30 percent share with the 30 percent concentrated in high-intent clusters is a better position than 60 percent concentrated in questions nobody buys from.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know a category is truly concentrated?

Count domains supplying half the citations across a fifty-prompt set. Three or fewer is concentrated; eight or more is open.

Should I write a comparison page naming the incumbent?

Yes, if it is accurate about both products and states where they are stronger. One-sided comparisons are recognisable and rarely cited.

How long does displacement take?

Months to quarters for citation, longer for recall. Recall responds to corroboration accumulated over time, not to publishing volume.
