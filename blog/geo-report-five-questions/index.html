The Five Questions a GEO Report Has to Answer

By IndexHot Team · November 28, 2026

Reports fail in a predictable way: they present numbers without a denominator, surfaces without a split, and a trend without a noise estimate. Five questions catch all of it, and they are the questions a sceptical reader asks in this order.

Question one: whose decision does this change

A report that does not name a decision is a newsletter. Naming it first forces the rest into shape: if the decision is which page to rewrite, the report needs cluster-level detail; if it is whether to continue the programme, it needs a cost per corrected defect.

Put the decision at the top, in one line. Readers who disagree can disagree with the decision rather than with the chart, which is a more productive argument.

Question two: what is the denominator

Every rate in the report needs its sample size printed beside it. Twelve of fifty and twenty-four of one hundred are both forty-eight percent and neither is the same claim. This is the single most common defect in AI visibility reporting, and it survives because percentages look finished.

Question three: is it split by surface and by branded or unbranded

Two splits, both mandatory. Surface, because a product that attaches twelve sources and one that attaches none cannot share a rate. Branded versus unbranded, because they measure recall and discovery, and the second is the one that predicts new demand.

A blended number across either split is not a summary. It is a different quantity, and reporting it as though it were the same invites exactly the wrong decision: adding brand mentions when the gap is in category questions.

Question four: what is the noise floor

State the run-to-run variation you measured on a frozen set. Without it, nobody can tell whether the change reported is a change. Two runs in the same week, same version, same prompts gives a number; that number is the bar any future movement has to clear.

Reports that include a noise floor are shorter, because half the movements they would otherwise have discussed turn out to be inside it.

Question five: what happens next, and what would make us stop

One next action, with an owner and a date. And one falsifier: the observation that would tell you this approach is not working in your category. A report without a falsifier is a commitment without an exit, which is how programmes outlive their evidence.

The five questions in order: which decision, what denominator, both splits, what noise, what next and what would stop us. If a report cannot answer all five in its first page, the remaining pages are decoration.
