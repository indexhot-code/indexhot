When an AI Answer Describes Your Brand Wrongly

By IndexHot Team · November 28, 2026

Someone forwards you an answer that gets your product, your pricing or your history wrong. There is no support queue for this, and the instinct to write a correcting blog post usually makes it worse. This is the order that works.

Four causes, and they look identical

What the answer saysMost likely causeFirst check
Describes an older product or name
Stale index, or two entities that never merged
Which URL is cited, and is it current

The instinct is to treat all four as a reputation problem. Three of them are data problems, and the fourth is a sentence problem. Fixing the wrong one is why teams spend a quarter publishing corrections that nothing reads.

What actually works, in order

  1. Correct the page that is cited, not a new page. A new page adds a fact; the cited page is the fact being used.
  2. Put the correction in the first sentence of the relevant section, with a date, so it is the sentence most likely to be lifted.
  3. If your own site already says the right thing and the answer still uses a third party, fix the third party: review profiles, directories, comparison sites, industry listings.
  4. Publish a short, dated corrections page only if the error is widely repeated. It gives the index something to resolve against, and it is the one legitimate use of the format.
  5. Re-run the exact prompt five times over two weeks before concluding anything. One bad answer is a sample, not a state.

The order matters because step one is the only step with a short feedback loop. Everything after it is either slower or depends on someone else's site.

What does not work

  • Emailing the provider to correct an answer. There is no queue, and no mechanism that would use one.
  • Writing a press release about the error. It reaches readers, not retrieval.
  • Adding schema that contradicts the page. Markup that disagrees with visible text is ignored or distrusted.
  • Publishing ten pages about the same correction. It reads as a campaign and dilutes the one page that should carry it.

If a competitor is quoted for a fact about you, the fix is usually to become the primary source for that fact again, not to argue that they are wrong. Corrections that begin with the other party's framing rarely get adopted.

The corrections page, done properly

It should state the claim that is circulating, the correct figure, the date the correction applies from, and the source. Four elements, no narrative. Keep it at a stable URL and update it rather than replacing it, so that a model resolving the discrepancy finds one current authoritative version instead of three versions of a correction.

It should not be titled as an open letter, and it should not argue. The format exists to be cited, which means the useful sentence has to be liftable in isolation: as of November 2026, the price is X, not Y.

How to know it worked

Track the specific prompt, the cited URL behind that sentence, and absorption: does the answer now contain your figure. Naming without absorption after a correction means the page was re-read and the sentence still did not survive, which sends you back to the sentence rather than to the outreach.
