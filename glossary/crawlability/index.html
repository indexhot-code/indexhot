What is Crawlability?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Crawlability is the degree to which automated crawlers can discover your URLs and fetch their content, determined by robots directives, internal links, server responses and rendering requirements.

In short: Whether crawlers can discover and fetch your pages.

Why it matters

Retrieval indexes are built by crawlers. A page that cannot be fetched cannot be cited, no matter how good it is. Most 'AI invisibility' cases we audit begin with a crawl or render problem, not a content problem.

How to work with it

Check robots.txt, status codes, sitemap coverage, internal link paths and whether core content exists in the initial HTML response.

Common mistakes

  • Blocking AI crawlers in robots.txt while expecting citations.
  • Relying on client-side rendering for primary content.
  • Leaving important pages reachable only through search or filters.

Real example

A site's CDN returned a JavaScript challenge to non-browser clients. Google indexed the pages normally, but AI crawlers received an empty shell, so citation rate stayed near zero despite good rankings. Whitelisting verified crawlers fixed it within days.

How to measure it

Fetch a page with curl and no JavaScript. If the body text is missing, assume at least one AI crawler sees the same empty response.

Where it breaks

Crawl access is binary but crawl frequency is not. A page can be crawlable and still fetched rarely, which slows every content change you make.

How it differs from adjacent terms

Indexability is the next gate; crawlability is whether a bot can fetch the page at all. Compared with a Canonical Tag, crawl access is binary while canonicalisation is a signal about which URL to prefer. A crawler that cannot fetch the page never reaches the canonical decision.

Quick checklist

  • Pages fetched with a plain client and no JavaScript.
  • robots.txt reviewed per user-agent, not globally.
  • CDN and WAF checked for challenges on bot traffic.
  • Status codes verified after every migration.
  • Sitemap entries matching live, crawlable URLs.

FAQ

Should I block AI crawlers?

That is a business decision. Blocking prevents training and retrieval use, which also removes you from AI answers. Many publishers allow retrieval while restricting training where the option exists.

How do I check whether AI crawlers can reach my site?

Fetch the page with a plain HTTP client and no JavaScript, then inspect robots.txt. If the content is missing or the path is disallowed, crawlers see the same thing.
