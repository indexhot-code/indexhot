What is Chunking?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Chunking is the process of dividing a document into smaller passages, typically a few sentences or a paragraph, so each can be embedded, retrieved and passed to a model independently.

In short: Splitting documents into passages for retrieval and embedding.

Why it matters

Chunk boundaries decide which part of your page can be found. If your answer spans three sections, no single chunk contains it, and a competing page with a compact answer wins the retrieval slot.

How to work with it

Give each section a descriptive heading, keep the answer inside one paragraph, and avoid splitting a claim across subsections.

Common mistakes

  • Writing answers that only make sense after reading the previous two sections.
  • Putting critical facts in a table that fragments badly when split.
  • Burying the direct answer in the middle of a long paragraph.

Real example

An answer to a warranty question quoted the wrong duration because the qualifying sentence sat in the following paragraph, outside the retrieved chunk. Merging the condition into the same sentence fixed the error permanently.

How to measure it

Go through your top pages and mark where a claim spans a heading boundary. Each of those boundaries is a place where the machine can lose half the meaning.

Where it breaks

Over-splitting also hurts. Paragraphs of one sentence each fragment the reasoning, so the model sees isolated facts with no relation between them.

How it differs from adjacent terms

Vector Embeddings measure similarity between chunks; chunking decides what a chunk is. Compared with Content Extractability, chunking is a mechanical property of the page while extractability is whether the resulting passage still makes sense alone. A page can be split cleanly and still produce chunks that are meaningless out of context.

Quick checklist

  • Each claim contained within one paragraph.
  • Headings describe the question the section answers.
  • Conditions and qualifiers inside the same sentence as the claim.
  • Tables accompanied by a plain-text summary.
  • Section length kept under roughly 150 words unless a list.

FAQ

What chunk size works best?

There is no universal number. The practical rule is one complete idea per passage, short enough to stand alone and long enough to be unambiguous.

Does chunking affect classic SEO?

Indirectly. Content organised into clear sections with descriptive headings tends to perform better for both readers and retrieval systems.
