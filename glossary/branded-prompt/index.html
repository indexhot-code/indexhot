What is Branded Prompt?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A branded prompt is a question in a measurement library that names the brand explicitly, such as asking what a specific product does or whether it is worth buying.

In short: A question that includes the brand name explicitly.

Why it matters

Branded prompts measure the model's knowledge of you rather than its willingness to recommend you. They are useful for detecting factual errors and gaps, but they inflate visibility metrics because the brand is a given.

How to work with it

Keep branded prompts in a separate bucket from non-branded ones, and code them for accuracy as well as presence.

Common mistakes

  • Blending branded and non-branded results into one headline score.
  • Ignoring factual errors in branded answers because the brand was mentioned.
  • Using only branded prompts and concluding visibility is healthy.

Real example

A brand scoring 92 percent on branded prompts scored 11 percent on non-branded ones. It knew its own name and was almost invisible to anyone who did not. The healthy top-line number described recall, not demand capture.

How to measure it

Code branded answers for factual accuracy as well as presence. Errors about your own pricing or features are the most damaging failures and the easiest to fix.

Where it breaks

Branded prompts reward existing awareness, so they are a weak test of growth. They should never dominate a library used to judge market position.

How it differs from adjacent terms

Non-Branded Prompts test recommendation; branded prompts test recall and accuracy. Compared with a Governed Question Library, a branded prompt is a single element, and its value depends on being a fixed, versioned part of that set. Used alone, branded prompts produce a flattering score that says nothing about whether new buyers would ever encounter the brand.

Quick checklist

  • No more than a fifth of the library branded.
  • Factual accuracy coded alongside presence.
  • Errors about pricing or features escalated as defects.
  • Branded and non-branded scores never averaged.
  • Prompt wording taken from real customer language.

FAQ

Why separate branded from non-branded prompts?

Because they answer different questions. Branded prompts test knowledge accuracy; non-branded prompts test whether you are recommended to someone who has never heard of you.

How many branded prompts are enough?

A common split is ten branded and forty non-branded in a fifty-prompt library, which gives enough coverage without letting branded queries dominate the score.
