What is Semantic Gap?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A semantic gap exists when a page describes the right thing in the wrong vocabulary, so neither lexical nor embedding matching connects it to the query. It is measured in meaning rather than keywords, and it is the reason well-written pages about the correct subject can be unreachable.

In short: The distance between the words a page uses and the meaning a query expresses.

Why it matters

Internal language is the usual cause. Teams describe their product in category terms that no buyer uses, and the page then matches nothing, because the buyer's phrasing never appears anywhere on it.

Gaps are also invisible in analytics: nothing looks broken, because the page is fine. It just never enters the candidate set.

How to work with it

List the phrases buyers use and check whether each appears in substance on the page.

  • Collect phrasing from sales calls, support tickets and search queries; these are the real vocabulary.
  • Add a plain-language sentence defining the product in buyer terms near the top of the page.
  • Use both vocabularies: the internal term for precision and the buyer term for reach.

Common mistakes

  • Optimising for the internal acronym, which matches the company's own vocabulary and nobody else's.
  • Assuming semantic search removes the need for exact phrasing; lexical matching is still half of hybrid retrieval.
  • Translating every synonym into a list, which produces keyword stuffing rather than a page that explains.

FAQ

Is this the same as keyword research?

Related but not identical. Keyword research finds volume; the semantic gap is about whether your page uses the vocabulary those queries actually contain.

How do I find it fastest?

Ask a new colleague or a customer to describe the product, and compare their words to the page's opening paragraph.
