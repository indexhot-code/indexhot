What is Knowledge Graph?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A knowledge graph is a structured database of entities, attributes and relationships, used by search and AI systems to understand that a brand, its products, people and locations are connected facts rather than strings.

In short: A structured network of entities and their relationships.

Why it matters

Knowledge graphs let a system answer questions about a brand without reading every page. If your entity is present and correct in the graph, facts propagate reliably; if it is absent or contradictory, each answer is a fresh guess.

How to work with it

Feed the graph through structured data, consistent profiles and corroborating third-party sources. Accuracy across sources matters more than volume in any single one.

Common mistakes

  • Publishing Organization markup that contradicts your directory listings.
  • Changing your legal name without updating external profiles.
  • Assuming a knowledge panel is required for good AI visibility.

Real example

A manufacturer changed its legal name in 2024 but left six directory listings untouched. Answers for two years alternated between the old and new names and cited an outdated address. Updating the listings moved the entity to a single consistent description.

How to measure it

Search your brand name across the major engines and directories, then compare name, address, website and founding year. Discrepancies are the graph's view of you, not a cosmetic issue.

Where it breaks

Graph correction is slow and indirect. There is no submission form, and conflicting third-party data can outvote your own site for months.

How it differs from adjacent terms

Schema Markup feeds a knowledge graph; the graph is the consumer. Compared with Entity Clarity, a graph is infrastructure while clarity is the observable outcome of being represented in it well. Contributing to a graph is indirect: you publish consistent facts and corroborating sources, then wait, which is why correcting a wrong attribute takes months rather than days.

Quick checklist

  • Name, address, website and founding year identical everywhere.
  • Directory listings audited at least twice a year.
  • Structured data values matching the visible page.
  • No outdated legal name left live on any profile.
  • Conflicting third-party pages identified and corrected where possible.

FAQ

Do I need a Google Knowledge Panel?

It helps but is not required. Consistent entity signals across your site and corroborating sources can support reliable answers without a panel.

How do I contribute to a knowledge graph?

Indirectly, through structured data, authoritative profiles and accurate third-party references. There is no direct submission form for most graphs.
