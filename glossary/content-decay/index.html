What is Content Decay?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Content decay is the erosion of a page's usefulness as a source: figures go stale, a competitor publishes a clearer version, and the page keeps its URL and rank while losing its role in answers.

In short: The gradual loss of citation and traffic as a page's facts age or as better-structured competitors appear.

Why it matters

Decay is invisible in most reporting because the URL still ranks and still receives traffic. The loss shows up in absorption and citation, which many teams do not measure separately.

It is also uneven. Pages carrying prices, limits or versions decay in months; definitional pages can last years unchanged.

How to work with it

Classify pages by volatility, then set a refresh cadence per class.

  • High volatility: prices, limits, availability, regulations. Review quarterly at minimum.
  • Medium: product capabilities, integrations, comparisons. Review twice a year.
  • Low: definitions, concepts, method explanations. Review when the concept changes.

Common mistakes

  • Refreshing the date without updating the numbers, which is detectable as unreliability rather than freshness.
  • Applying one cadence to the whole site, which over-serves evergreen pages and under-serves volatile ones.
  • Measuring decay by traffic alone, which misses pages that still rank but no longer get used in answers.

FAQ

How do I detect decay early?

Watch citation rate on the affected cluster rather than page traffic. Citations fall before rankings do.

Is rewriting always the right response?

No. Updating the specific claim that went stale is usually enough, and a full rewrite resets the corroboration the page had accumulated.
