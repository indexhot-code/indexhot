What is Passage Retrieval?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Passage retrieval is the step where a system selects sections of documents rather than whole pages. Instead of asking which page answers a question, it asks which paragraph does, which changes what a page has to do to be useful.

In short: Finding the specific section of a page, rather than the page as a whole, that answers part of a query.

How it differs from page-level retrieval

Classic search ranks documents. Passage retrieval splits documents into chunks, embeds them, and ranks chunks against the query separately. A single page can therefore contribute three passages to three different queries, and two of its sections can compete against each other.

The consequence for content work is that the unit of optimisation shrinks. A page no longer needs to be the best page on a topic; it needs to contain the best passage for a specific question, which is a much cheaper thing to arrange.

What makes a passage retrievable

  • A heading that names its subject, so the chunk arrives with a label rather than mid-argument.
  • A first sentence that states the claim without pronouns pointing backwards.
  • Length in the range a chunker will not split: long enough to be specific, short enough not to be averaged away.
  • Specific nouns and quantities, since vague passages match vague queries only.

Why long pages can lose to short ones

A page of four thousand words split into forty chunks dilutes its own signal: each chunk competes with the others for the same query, and the system picks one. A page of eight hundred words on one question produces two or three strong chunks that do not compete with anything.

This is not an argument for thin pages. It is an argument for pages organised so that each section answers one question completely, whatever the page's total length.

The measurement implication

Because retrieval is passage-level, a citation tells you which section was used, not merely which domain. Recording the cited URL and the sentence the answer borrowed is more informative than recording the domain alone.

It also explains a common confusion: a page can be cited for a question it answers in one paragraph and ignored for a question it discusses at length in another. The page is not inconsistent; retrieval selected a different chunk.

Frequently asked questions

Is passage retrieval the same as chunking?

Chunking is the preparation step that splits documents; passage retrieval is the selection step that ranks the resulting chunks against a query. You control only the first.

Should I shorten my pages?

Not necessarily. Split long pages into question-shaped sections with self-contained opening sentences, and let the chunker do the rest.

Does this affect classic search?

Marginally. Google ranks pages, so page-level quality still matters; the passage-level effect is concentrated on answer surfaces.
