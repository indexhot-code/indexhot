What is Non-Branded Prompt?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A non-branded prompt is a category or problem question that does not name a brand, such as asking for the best tool for a task or how to solve a common problem.

In short: A category question asked without naming any brand.

Why it matters

Non-branded prompts are where new demand actually converts. They reveal whether an assistant recommends you to a buyer who has never heard of you, which is the growth-relevant half of AI visibility.

How to work with it

Build them from real buyer language: problem statements, comparison intents, budget constraints and use-case specifics, not from your own product taxonomy.

Common mistakes

  • Writing prompts in marketing language instead of buyer language.
  • Covering only head terms and ignoring specific constraints.
  • Rewriting prompts each cycle so trends are not comparable.

Real example

A niche analytics tool scored zero across all forty non-branded prompts in its first run. Adding four comparison pages and a category-definition page produced mentions in nine, with three citations, within seven weeks.

How to measure it

Write prompts in buyer language from support tickets and sales calls, not from your product taxonomy. If the wording sounds like your homepage, it is not a real prompt.

Where it breaks

Non-branded categories are often locked by directories and review sites. Zero is frequently a distribution problem, not a content problem, and needs partnerships rather than more pages.

How it differs from adjacent terms

Branded Prompts measure what the model already knows about you; non-branded prompts measure whether it would introduce you. Compared with Share of Voice, non-branded prompts are the input and share is one output computed from them. A library weighted toward branded prompts will report strong visibility for a brand that no new buyer would ever find.

Quick checklist

  • At least four fifths of the library non-branded.
  • Prompts cover problem, comparison, budget and use-case intents.
  • Wording sourced from tickets and calls rather than the website.
  • Zero-score categories investigated for distribution causes.
  • Same prompt set reused across every platform and cycle.

FAQ

How many non-branded prompts should a library have?

Forty out of fifty is a practical default, covering problem, comparison, budget and use-case intents across the category.

Why do I score zero on non-branded prompts?

Usually because the category is dominated by established sources, or because your pages do not state category-level claims a model can ground on. Both are fixable.
