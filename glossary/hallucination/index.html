What is Hallucination?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A hallucination is a model output that is stated confidently but has no basis in the retrieved sources or the underlying data, such as an invented feature, price or partnership.

In short: Confidently stated output with no basis in the sources.

Why it matters

For brands, hallucination is a reputational and commercial risk. An AI answer that misstates your pricing or attributes a competitor's product to you reaches buyers before your site does, and there is no correction form.

How to work with it

Hallucination risk falls when authoritative, consistent, well-structured pages state the facts explicitly. Ambiguity and contradiction invite invention.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving key facts implicit, so the model fills gaps itself.
  • Publishing outdated figures that contradict third-party sources.
  • Using inconsistent product or entity names across pages.

Real example

An AI answer described a mid-size CRM as offering an enterprise SSO tier it did not sell. The error traced to a two-year-old pricing page still indexed and never marked as superseded. Removing the page and publishing a dated replacement corrected the answer in most surfaces.

How to measure it

Ask ten identity questions about your brand monthly, such as what you sell, who you serve and what you charge. Any contradiction with your own site is a live hallucination risk, not a curiosity.

Where it breaks

Correction is slow and incomplete. Deletion removes a source, but cached copies and third-party repeats can keep the error alive for months.

How it differs from adjacent terms

Grounding is the defence; hallucination is the failure it defends against. Compared with Entity Clarity, hallucination is broader: an entity can be perfectly resolved and still be described with invented facts, which is why accuracy must be measured separately from identity. Schema Markup reduces ambiguity about structured attributes, but it does not prevent invented prose about things the page never states.

Quick checklist

  • Ten identity questions asked monthly across all engines.
  • Every contradiction logged with the page that caused it.
  • Archived pages taken down rather than left to rot.
  • Structured data values matching visible copy exactly.
  • Third-party directories audited for outdated facts.

FAQ

Can I correct an AI hallucination about my brand?

There is no direct correction channel. The practical route is to publish clear, consistent, well-structured facts and strengthen the sources models already trust.

Does structured data reduce hallucination?

It reduces ambiguity about facts and entities, which lowers the chance of invention, but it does not eliminate the risk entirely.
