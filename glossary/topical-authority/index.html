What is Topical Authority?

Topical authority is the depth, breadth and consistency with which a site covers a subject, such that a search or AI system treats it as a reliable source for that topic rather than a generalist.

In short: Depth and consistency of coverage across a topic.

Why it matters

Retrieval systems choose among candidates for a sub-query. A site with a complete cluster around the topic is a safer choice than a site with one isolated page, because the surrounding coverage corroborates the claim.

How to work with it

Build hub-and-spoke clusters: one definitive overview, dedicated pages for each sub-question, and links that make the relationship explicit.

Common mistakes

  • Publishing many thin posts on unrelated topics instead of a cluster.
  • Creating a hub with no dedicated sub-pages, or sub-pages with no hub.
  • Leaving clusters unlinked, so the relationship exists only in your planning document.

FAQ

How many pages make a topic cluster?

There is no fixed number. The test is completeness: can a reader, or a retrieval system, get every sub-question answered without leaving the cluster?

Does topical authority replace domain authority?

They reinforce each other. Topical coverage helps a site compete for a specific subject even when its overall domain strength is lower.
