What is Grounding?

Grounding is the process of anchoring a model's output to verifiable external sources, so that claims in the answer can be traced back to a document rather than invented.

In short: Anchoring generated output to verifiable external sources.

Why it matters

Grounding is what separates a citable answer from a plausible guess. Systems that ground well produce answers with attached sources; systems that ground poorly hallucinate. For brands, being the grounding source is the strongest form of inclusion.

How to work with it

Grounded answers usually show inline citations or a source list. The passages used tend to be factual, dated and unambiguous.

Common mistakes

  • Publishing claims with no traceable evidence, which gives the model nothing safe to ground on.
  • Contradicting your own pages, which makes grounding unreliable.
  • Assuming a link in the answer guarantees your wording was used.

FAQ

Is grounding the same as citing?

Related but distinct. Grounding is the mechanism that ties output to sources; citation is the visible attribution of that source in the answer.

What makes a page easy to ground on?

Specific, verifiable claims stated in plain text, with dates and numbers attached, and consistent with the rest of your site.

Related terms
