What is Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an architecture in which a language model retrieves relevant documents from an external index before generating a response, using those documents as evidence.

In short: Retrieving external documents before generating an answer.

Why it matters

RAG is the reason a brand can appear in an AI answer at all. Without retrieval, the model answers from training data alone and your recent content is irrelevant. With retrieval, the quality of your retrievable passages determines whether you are used.

How to work with it

A typical pipeline embeds documents into chunks, retrieves candidates by similarity, re-ranks them, then conditions generation on the top passages.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming training-data presence is enough and ignoring fresh retrieval.
  • Publishing content that only works as a whole document, not as passages.
  • Blocking crawlers that feed retrieval indexes.

FAQ

Does RAG mean my content is read live?

Often yes, but not always. Some systems retrieve from a live index, others from a cached crawl. Either way, crawlable, well-chunked pages are the prerequisite.

Why does my page get retrieved but not quoted?

Retrieval and generation are separate steps. A page can be fetched as a candidate and then discarded because another passage summarises the answer more cleanly.

Related terms
