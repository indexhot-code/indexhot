What is Claim Verifiability?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Claim verifiability is the property that lets an outside party confirm a statement: a defined measure, a stated sample, a named source, or a fact that can be observed. It decides whether an answer repeats your sentence or paraphrases around it.

In short: Whether someone outside the company could check the statement.

The cheapest way to make a claim verifiable

State the measure, the period and the scope in the same sentence. Not we are fast, but median reconciliation latency of 60 seconds across 3,000 accounts in Q3 2026. The second version is checkable in principle by anyone who can see the product, which is what makes it repeatable.

What verifiability does not mean

It does not mean publishing everything, and it does not mean third-party audit. It means the claim is stated in a form where checking is conceivable; a model treats conceivable checking differently from an assertion it must take on trust.

Interaction with absorption

Verifiable claims are the raw material of claim-level absorption, and they are also what makes recommendation-level absorption possible: an answer recommends what it can justify. A page of verifiable specifics supports a recommendation; a page of superlatives supports a paraphrase.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a third-party audit to be verifiable?

No. A stated measure and period is enough for the claim to be checkable in principle.

Are customer counts verifiable?

Only with a definition and a date. A bare count is a superlative in numeric clothing.

What about confidential figures?

State a range and the constraint instead. A bounded range is checkable; a withheld number is not a claim.
