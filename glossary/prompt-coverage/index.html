What is Prompt Coverage?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Prompt coverage measures how much of the buying path a library samples, rather than how many prompts it contains. Fifty prompts concentrated on definitions can cover a tenth of the decision and report a healthy rate for it.

In short: How much of the buying path your prompt library actually samples.

Coverage is not count

A library of fifty prompts sounds substantial until you classify them: if forty are definitional, the comparative, recommendational and transactional stages are unsampled. The report is accurate about one stage and silent about three, and silence reads as absence of a problem.

Coverage is therefore a map, not a total. The useful output is a grid: stages on one axis, roles or locales on the other, with a count in each cell and a zero where nothing was asked.

Why the last two stages are usually empty

Verify and switch are operationally uncomfortable: they invite questions about price, contract length and migration cost that marketing pages avoid. They are also where the decision actually closes, and where a competitor's documentation page can quietly win the account.

Coverage and the gap number

An unbranded visibility gap computed on a library that covers only the define stage is a gap in definitions, not in the category. Before attributing a gap to content quality, check whether the stages where you are weak were ever sampled.

Frequently asked questions

How many prompts is enough?

Enough to cover every stage and role you sell to, at five or more per cell. Total count follows from the grid, not the other way round.

Should I add prompts or replace them?

Replace within a stage when it is saturated; add when a cell is empty.

Does coverage affect comparability?

Only if you change it mid-period. Version the library and re-baseline when the grid changes.
