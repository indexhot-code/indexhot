What is Source Concentration?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Source concentration is the distribution of cited domains across a prompt set. High concentration means a small set of sites supplies most citations, so entry requires displacing an incumbent; low concentration means the citation pool is open.

In short: How few domains account for most citations in a category's answers.

Why it matters

Concentration determines strategy. In a concentrated category, publishing more pages changes little and the work is to become one of the few sources the model trusts; in an open category, coverage breadth wins.

It is also a useful early signal for a new category: where concentration is still low, the cost of entry is low, and that window closes as the incumbents accumulate citations.

How to work with it

Collect the cited domains across your prompt set and count how many supply 50 and 80 percent of citations.

  • Record unique domains per prompt and the top three domains overall.
  • Compare your own share of citations to the concentration curve; being fourth in a market of four is different from being fourth in a market of forty.
  • Re-measure quarterly; concentration rises as a category matures.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring only your own citation rate without the denominator, so you cannot tell whether the category is open.
  • Assuming a concentrated category is unwinnable; concentration often reflects one site answering one question well, which is replicable.
  • Ignoring that surfaces concentrate differently, so a category can be open on one and closed on another.

FAQ

What counts as high concentration?

As a rule of thumb, if three domains supply more than half the citations across a fifty-prompt set, treat the category as concentrated.

Does concentration differ by platform?

Yes. Surfaces that attach long source lists look less concentrated than those citing one or two pages, even when the same sites dominate.
