What is Crawl Trap?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A crawl trap is a pattern that generates effectively unlimited URLs from a small content set: faceted filters, sort parameters, calendar archives, session IDs. The pages are technically valid and individually worthless, and they consume the capacity that would have gone to the pages that matter.

In short: Near-infinite generated URLs that consume crawl capacity without adding indexable value.

Four common traps

  • Faceted filters. Colour × size × price × brand combinations, each a URL.
  • Calendars and pagination. Every date and every page number, including empty ones.
  • Parameters that do not change content. Sort order, tracking tags, session identifiers.
  • Search result pages. Internal search URLs exposed to crawlers, generating thousands of thin pages.

Why it matters more on answer surfaces

Capacity is finite per site. When a large share of fetches resolve to parameter variants of the same three pages, the new pages and the pages that answer questions are fetched less. The site is not penalised; it is simply read less, which is indistinguishable from neglect in a visibility report.

The fix set

  1. Disallow or canonicalise parameter combinations, and keep the one combination worth indexing.
  2. Noindex empty result and empty calendar pages, and return 404 for the ones that do not exist.
  3. Keep internal search results out of the crawl path and out of the sitemap.
  4. Check the log monthly for URL patterns whose fetch count is high and whose citation count is zero.

The diagnostic is a ratio, not a rule: fetches per cited URL. A section with thousands of fetches and no citations is either unindexable, uncitable, or a trap.

What it looks like in a report

Nothing, usually. Traps do not produce errors, they produce silence. The only place they are visible is the server log, which is why a quarterly log read is worth more than another dashboard.

Frequently asked questions

Does noindex solve a trap?

It stops indexing, not fetching. You usually need disallow or canonicalisation as well.

Are paginated pages a trap?

Only when they are deep and unlinked. A handful of paginated pages with clear links is normal.

How do I know I have one?

Compare fetch counts to citations per URL pattern over a month. The pattern with high fetches and no citations is the suspect.
