What is Provenance Signal?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A provenance signal is anything that lets a system verify where a claim came from: a dated primary source, an author with a track record, a methodology page, or a dataset with a version. Answers repeat claims they can trace and hedge ones they cannot.

In short: Evidence that a claim, number or page originates where it says it does, which is what makes it safe to repeat.

Why models care about origin

Repeating a specific claim is a risk. If the claim turns out to be wrong, the answer is wrong. Models therefore prefer claims that arrive with evidence of origin, because the evidence transfers some of the accountability to a source.

This is why a number on a page with a stated method is quoted, and the same number on a page with no method is paraphrased into something vague. The number did not change; its traceability did.

What counts as a signal

  • A primary source, linked, with its own date: filing, standard, dataset, study.
  • A method section that says how the number was produced and what it excludes.
  • A visible author or organisation with a page explaining who they are.
  • A version and as-of date on any dataset you publish.
  • Corroboration elsewhere: the same figure, from you, on a third-party page.

What does not count

Citations to yourself, statistics with no denominator, and numbers attributed to research nobody can open. These read as decoration and are treated as such: the claim stays unquoted while the surrounding prose gets summarised.

The cheap version

One paragraph per volatile number: where it came from, when, over what sample, and what it does not cover. That paragraph is often the difference between a quoted statistic and an unquoted one, and it takes twenty minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Does linking to a source transfer trust?

It transfers verifiability. That is what makes the claim repeatable.

Do I need original research?

No. Clear provenance of existing figures is usually enough, and easier than producing new data.

Is a methodology page enough on its own?

Only for numbers produced under it. Each claim still needs its own origin.
