What is Zero-Mention Prompt?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A zero-mention prompt is the absence case, and it is the most informative cell in a measurement sheet. It states that the model did not reach the brand, and the cluster it sits in tells you which stage failed: reach, eligibility or usability.

In short: A prompt where the brand appears nowhere in the answer, not even as a passing name.

Why it matters

Aggregates hide absence. A 60 percent mention rate can mean broad presence or a split of forty prompts fully covered and forty entirely missed, and the two require opposite responses.

Zero-mention prompts are also the honest denominator for entry. If nine of ten discovery prompts return nothing, publishing another article is unlikely to fix it, because the problem is upstream of content.

How to work with it

Code zero-mention separately from low mention, and group zeros by cluster.

  • A zero in a discovery cluster points to reach or entity clarity.
  • A zero in an implementation cluster usually points to a missing page rather than a missing signal.
  • A cluster that is all zeros across surfaces is a structural problem, not a writing one.

Common mistakes

  • Reporting only the aggregate rate and never listing the zeros.
  • Treating a zero as a content gap without checking whether the page is fetchable.
  • Ignoring zeros that sit next to a competitor's strong presence, which is the most actionable kind.

FAQ

Is zero mention always bad?

Not always: some queries are outside the brand's category and should be zero. The useful test is whether a competitor is present in the same answer.

How many zeros are normal?

In a fifty-prompt set for a small brand, twenty to thirty is common. Below ten usually means the library is skewed toward branded prompts.
