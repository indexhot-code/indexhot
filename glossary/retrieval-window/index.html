What is Retrieval Window?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

The retrieval window is the practical boundary a system applies when choosing sources: how recent they must be for time-sensitive questions, how many results per sub-query, and which domains count as eligible. It is not published, but it is observable.

In short: The recency and scope limits within which a retrieval system will accept sources.

Why it matters

A correct page outside the window is not retrieved. For pricing, regulations and product availability, the window is narrow and stale pages are excluded regardless of quality.

Observing the window tells you what freshness cadence actually matters for your category, instead of publishing on a schedule nobody consumes.

How to work with it

Infer the window from the sources that appear for time-sensitive prompts.

  • Run a dated prompt and note the publication years of the cited pages; the oldest recurring source approximates the boundary.
  • Refresh pages that carry volatile facts: prices, limits, versions, dates.
  • Put the date visibly on the page rather than only in the sitemap, since extraction reads the page.

Common mistakes

  • Setting one refresh cadence for the whole site, which over-serves evergreen pages and under-serves volatile ones.
  • Updating the date without updating the numbers, which is detected as unreliable rather than fresh.
  • Assuming recency outweighs specificity; a fresh page saying nothing loses to an older page with a number.

FAQ

Does freshness help every query?

No. For stable concepts, age is irrelevant and specificity dominates. For price and regulation questions it is decisive.

How often should volatile pages be refreshed?

When the fact changes, and at least quarterly for pages that state prices or limits. Date-stamping without correcting is worse than leaving it alone.
