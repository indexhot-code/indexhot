What is Content Gating?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Content gating is any barrier between a URL and its text: a login, a lead form, a paywall, a consent interstitial. Gating is a legitimate business choice and a retrieval decision at the same time, and most sites make it without noticing the second part.

In short: Restricting content behind login, forms, paywalls or consent walls, which removes it from retrieval unless the restriction is narrowly scoped.

Three gates, three different effects

  • Hard gate. No content without authentication. Content is invisible to retrieval, and the URL may still be indexed with an empty snippet.
  • Metered gate. Content visible until a threshold. Usually readable by a first fetch, which is what retrieval does.
  • Form gate. Content visible but covered by a modal. Readability depends entirely on whether the text is in the HTML rather than loaded afterwards.

The pattern that works

Gate the asset, not the argument. Give the reasoning, definitions and specifications away, and gate the template, the dataset export, the calculator output. The section that would be quoted is rarely the section customers pay for.

This is not charity. A gated PDF is invisible; the page that explains what is in it is quotable and brings the reader to the gate.

What to check

  1. Fetch a gated URL with no cookies and read what remains.
  2. Confirm the visible text is in the HTML, not injected after a form submit.
  3. Confirm the consent wall is not blocking the body for first-time clients.
  4. Confirm gated URLs are not in the sitemap hoping to be indexed for content nobody can read.

The trade-off, stated plainly

Full gating buys lead capture and costs every citation the page would have earned. For most B2B sites the citation is worth more than the form, because the form converts people who already arrived, and the citation is how they arrive.

Frequently asked questions

Can gated pages still be cited?

Rarely, and only through secondary sources that describe them. The page itself contributes nothing to the answer.

Is a metered paywall safe?

Mostly. First fetches usually see the content, which is the fetch that matters for retrieval.

Should gated URLs be in the sitemap?

Only if some version is publicly readable. Otherwise you are advertising pages a crawler cannot use.
