What is AI Crawler?

An AI crawler is an automated agent operated by a model provider to fetch web content, either to build training data or to serve live retrieval for answers, identified in server logs by a specific user-agent.

In short: A bot that fetches content for model training or live retrieval.

Why it matters

Crawlers decide whether your content exists in the system at all. Blocking them is a legitimate business choice, but blocking them accidentally, through a broad robots rule or a firewall, is the most common cause of a site being invisible in AI answers despite excellent content.

How to work with it

Review robots.txt rules per user-agent, distinguish training crawlers from retrieval crawlers where the provider offers separate tokens, and audit logs for 403s against known bot user-agents.

Common mistakes

  • Blocking all bots with a single wildcard rule during a staging setup and never reversing it.
  • Assuming a WAF or CDN challenge does not affect legitimate crawlers.
  • Treating training access and retrieval access as the same decision.

FAQ

Should I allow AI crawlers?

That is a strategic decision. Blocking removes you from both training and retrieval, which also removes you from answers. Many publishers allow retrieval while restricting training where the provider exposes separate controls.

How do I verify an AI crawler can reach my pages?

Fetch a page with a plain HTTP client and no JavaScript, check the response code, then compare robots.txt against the specific user-agent. If the text is missing or blocked, the crawler sees the same.
