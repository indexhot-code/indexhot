What is Entity Resolution?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Entity resolution is the process by which a system decides that Acme Ltd, Acme Labs and acme.com are one organisation, or decides that they are not. Failing it produces fragments: several weak entities instead of one strong one, and an answer that hedges about all of them.

In short: Deciding whether several names, profiles or domains refer to one organisation.

What the system uses to decide

  • Name similarity, including suffixes, spacing and abbreviations.
  • Shared identifiers: domain, logo, address, registration number, founding date.
  • Co-occurrence: whether the same pages, people and products appear across the records.
  • Declared links: sameAs, official profiles, cross-site references.

Where resolution fails in practice

Three patterns account for most failures: a rename without aliases, a sub-brand sharing the parent's name, and a legal entity that differs from the trading name. In each case the pages are individually correct and jointly ambiguous.

The symptom is specific: answers describe the company accurately and attribute the description to the wrong URL, or split a product line across two names. Reporting this as a citation problem sends teams to the wrong fix.

What improves it

  1. Declare one canonical name and use it in the first sentence of the homepage and the about page.
  2. Record aliases explicitly: former names, legal names, abbreviations, and local-language forms.
  3. Keep sameAs accurate and short; a dormant profile weakens the set it belongs to.
  4. Give products their own resolvable identities rather than reusing the parent name.
  5. Check whether a same-named company exists in your category, and disambiguate in text if so.

How to test your own

Ask a fresh assistant who you are, twice, in different sessions, and compare the company description and the cited URL. Consistent description plus consistent citation means resolution is holding. Description consistent, citation varying between two URLs usually means an unresolved duplicate.

Frequently asked questions

Is entity resolution something I can see?

Only indirectly, through the symptoms: split descriptions, mismatched citations, products attributed to the wrong brand.

Does sameAs fix a rename?

It helps if the old name remains a documented alias. Declaring a link without recording the alias leaves the old record orphaned.

What if a competitor has a similar name?

Disambiguate in text: state the category, the location and one distinguishing fact in the same sentence as your name.
