What is Brand Recall?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Brand recall is unprompted mention. The prompt describes a need and never names the brand; if the answer still introduces it, the model holds the brand strongly enough to surface it. This is the closest observable proxy for prior knowledge.

In short: Whether a model names a brand without being prompted by its name, from memory rather than retrieval.

Why it matters

Recall is the hardest signal to move and the most valuable when it appears, because it means the brand is part of the model's picture of the category rather than a search result.

It is also the most sensitive to entity work. A brand that is consistently described in the same terms across the web, with a clean entity page and corroborating schema, is more likely to be recalled than one whose description varies by source.

How to work with it

Use non-branded prompts only, and code whether the brand appears without its name in the prompt.

  • Separate recall from retrieval-assisted mention: if the site is cited, retrieval did the work.
  • Recall without citation is the strongest state and usually indicates training-data presence.
  • Track recall over quarters, not weeks; it moves slowly and noisy weekly data invites false conclusions.

Common mistakes

  • Calling any non-branded mention "recall" when the answer also cites the site, which is retrieval.
  • Expecting recall to respond to publishing cadence; it responds to consistency and corroboration over time.
  • Ignoring negative recall, where the brand is recalled with an outdated or wrong description.

FAQ

Can recall be measured reliably?

Only approximately. Answers vary, and a brand recalled in one run may be absent in the next; use three runs and report a range.

What most improves recall?

Consistent entity description across owned and third-party sources, plus enough independent coverage that the model has repeated evidence.
