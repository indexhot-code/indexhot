What is Answer Coverage?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Answer coverage is breadth: how much of the question space the brand occupies. It differs from mention rate in weighting, because coverage counts each cluster once rather than weighting by volume, and it is the metric that best predicts whether a buyer can encounter the brand at any stage.

In short: The share of a category's prompt set in which the brand appears at all, regardless of depth.

Why it matters

A brand can be strongly cited on two prompts and absent from forty. Coverage exposes that shape, while an average rate conceals it.

It is also the fairest comparison across brands of different sizes, because it asks how much of the space is occupied rather than how often a single question is won.

How to work with it

Count clusters with at least one mention, then report coverage per cluster.

  • Cluster coverage: how many of the eight intent clusters contain any mention.
  • Depth: within a covered cluster, how many prompts produce a mention.
  • Report both, because coverage without depth is a presence that never converts.

Common mistakes

  • Reporting only the aggregate rate, which mixes coverage and depth into an unreadable number.
  • Counting a single mention in a cluster as full coverage.
  • Comparing coverage across library sizes without normalising.

FAQ

Is coverage more important than rate?

They answer different questions. Coverage asks whether a buyer can meet you at any stage; rate asks how often. A programme needs both, reported separately.

What is a good coverage number?

For a mid-sized brand, half the clusters with at least one mention is a workable starting point. All eight covered with thin depth is common and fragile.
