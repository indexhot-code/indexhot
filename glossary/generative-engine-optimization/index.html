What is Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of structuring a brand's content and entity signals so that generative engines such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews mention it, cite it as a source and carry its claims into the answer text.

In short: The practice of making a brand visible, citable and quotable inside AI-generated answers.

Why it matters

Search behaviour has shifted from scanning a list of links to reading a single synthesised answer. That answer names only a handful of sources, so being absent from it means being absent from the buyer's decision. GEO exists because the rules for earning that presence differ from classic ranking: retrieval, grounding and extractability matter as much as authority.

How to work with it

GEO work usually starts with a fixed question library, a baseline measurement across platforms, then content and entity fixes ranked by where the gaps are largest.

Common mistakes

  • Treating GEO as keyword optimization with a new label, and ignoring retrieval and citation mechanics.
  • Optimising for mentions while never checking whether the brand is actually cited or absorbed.
  • Measuring once, then optimising blind without a reproducible baseline.

Real example

A 40-person B2B analytics vendor had strong organic rankings but appeared in 4 of 50 tracked prompts. After publishing a comparison page and adding Organization markup with consistent product names, mentions rose to 31 of 50 over eight weeks, and the domain appeared as a cited source in 19. The change came from clarity, not volume: total published words rose by only 12 percent.

How to measure it

Run the same 50 prompts before and after any change, on all four surfaces, and record mention, citation and absorption separately. Comparing one blended score hides which of the three moved and why.

Where it breaks

GEO does not override demand. If nobody asks the question your page answers, no amount of structuring will produce an answer citation. Check prompt volume before optimising a topic.

How it differs from adjacent terms

Answer Engine Optimization overlaps heavily but is narrower in origin: AEO grew out of featured snippets and voice answers, where one page is extracted intact. GEO assumes the answer is synthesised from several sources and may never quote you verbatim. Compared with AI Search Visibility, which is the measurement of the outcome, GEO is the set of interventions that move it. Practitioners often run AEO formatting and GEO entity work together, because extractability without entity clarity produces snippets about a company the model cannot identify.

Quick checklist

  • One canonical brand name and domain, used identically everywhere.
  • Every target question answered in a self-contained opening sentence.
  • Organization and product markup present and consistent with visible text.
  • A fixed prompt library run on all four surfaces before any change.
  • Mention, citation and absorption reported separately, never blended.
  • At least five internal links into every page you want cited.

FAQ

Is GEO different from SEO?

They overlap but are not the same. SEO competes for a ranking position; GEO competes for inclusion in a generated answer, which depends on retrieval, grounding and how easily your content can be summarised.

Do I need a technical team to do GEO?

Most gains come from content structure, entity consistency and structured data. A small team can run a baseline and fix the highest-impact gaps without custom infrastructure.
