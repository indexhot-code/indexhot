What is Multi-turn Context?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Multi-turn context is the set of earlier turns a system carries forward. A follow-up question is interpreted relative to what was already said, so the same words can retrieve different sources depending on the conversation that preceded them.

In short: The accumulated conversation state that shapes how a later question is interpreted and answered.

Why it matters

It means a brand's presence early in a conversation has persistent value: later turns elaborate within the entity set already established, rather than building a new one.

It also means isolated prompt measurement can overstate or understate reality, because real usage is conversational and the first turn sets much of the trajectory.

How to work with it

Simulate two-turn and three-turn conversations rather than isolated prompts.

  • Record the answer at each turn and note whether sources narrow, hold or change.
  • Check whether an entity named at turn one survives to turn three.
  • Test whether a brand absent at turn one can be introduced by a later, more specific question.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring single prompts and assuming the numbers describe conversational use.
  • Treating a follow-up as an independent search, which ignores that the earlier answer constrains retrieval.
  • Ignoring that a wrong fact introduced at turn one propagates through later turns.

FAQ

Can a brand recover if absent at turn one?

Sometimes, if a later turn is specific enough to trigger fresh retrieval. Recovery is unreliable, which is why first-turn presence matters.

Do all surfaces carry context?

Most chat surfaces do within a session. Search-like surfaces with independent queries do not, so the same prompt behaves differently.
