What is Crawl Log?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

A crawl log lists requests by crawler, URL, timestamp and response status. It is the primary evidence for whether crawl budget is being wasted, and it is the only place where soft 404 behaviour shows up as a pattern rather than as an anecdote.

In short: The record of which URLs a crawler requested and what each returned.

Why it matters

Sites rarely fail uniformly. They fail in clusters: a section of the site returns 200 with no unique content, or a parameter pattern multiplies URLs, and the log is where that becomes visible as a share of total requests.

The log also distinguishes discovery problems from eligibility problems. A URL never requested is a linking issue; a URL requested monthly and never indexed is a content or status issue.

How to work with it

Read the log by response code share and by section.

  • A high share of 404 or 301 responses means budget is spent on URLs that cannot be indexed.
  • A high share of 200 responses with duplicate content means soft 404 is inflating the crawlable set.
  • URLs requested once and never again usually indicate low internal link weight.

Common mistakes

  • Reading only total requests, which says nothing about efficiency.
  • Assuming a small site cannot waste crawl; a 145-page site with fifty soft 404s wastes a third of its allowance.
  • Ignoring the crawler identity, when different crawlers behave differently on the same URL.

FAQ

Where do I find a crawl log?

Search Console exposes crawl statistics by response code. Server logs give the per-URL detail if you have access.

What share of non-200 is acceptable?

Under ten percent for a healthy static site. Above twenty percent means the crawlable set exceeds the indexable set.
