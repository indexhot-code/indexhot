Core concepts

The vocabulary of the field before the measurements: what a generative engine is, what it optimises for, and which surfaces are being counted.

These entry points are the ones a team needs before it can run a measurement at all. Each defines a term that appears in every later report, which is why they are grouped first rather than scattered through the set.

Where two terms overlap, the distinction is usually the useful part. Answer engine optimisation and generative engine optimisation are often used interchangeably in vendor copy, and the difference is what each is measured against.

Terms in this category

Generative Engine Optimization the practice of making a brand visible, citable and quotable inside AI-generated answers Answer Engine Optimization optimising content to be chosen as the direct answer, not just a ranked result AI Search Visibility how often, how prominently and how deeply a brand appears in AI answers Answer Engine a search system that returns a direct answer instead of a list of links Generative Search search that synthesises a new answer from retrieved sources Zero-Click Search queries resolved on the results or answer surface without a visit Google AI Overviews the AI-generated summary block at the top of Google results AI Snippet a short machine-generated passage lifted from sources to answer a query Answer Format The shape an AI answer takes: prose, a ranked list, a comparison table or a set of steps. Query Refinement The follow-up questions a user asks after an initial AI answer, and the second retrieval they trigger. Competitor Set The specific brands an AI answer names as alternatives for a given query. GEO Audit A structured review of why a site is absent from AI answers, run in stage order rather than content order. Zero-Mention Prompt A prompt where the brand appears nowhere in the answer, not even as a passing name.

Frequently asked questions

Where should a beginner start?

GEO, answer engine optimisation and AI search visibility, in that order, since each is defined in terms of the others.

Are these definitions official?

No. They are the definitions IndexHot uses in its measurement standard, published so that a report citing them means one specific thing.

Why group terms into categories?

Because the set is now large enough that alphabetical browsing stops helping; category browsing shows which part of the problem a term belongs to.
