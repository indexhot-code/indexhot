Entity and content

Two things a machine must resolve before it can use you: what you are, and whether what you wrote can be lifted and repeated.

The entity half covers identity: names, categories, profiles, markup and the consistency between them. The content half covers quotability: structure, extractability, provenance and whether a sentence survives being cut from its page.

They fail differently and are fixed by different people. A split entity is a data problem across profiles; an unquotable paragraph is an editing problem on one page.

Frequently asked questions

Does schema fix an unclear brand?

It expresses clarity if the clarity exists. Markup cannot supply a category you have never stated.

What is the cheapest content fix in this group?

Restating the claim in the first sentence of a section, with a number and a date, so it can be lifted without context.

Why include both in one category?

Because a system must resolve both before it can cite a passage, and teams tend to see only the half they own.
