What is Citation Source Diversity?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Citation source diversity is the degree to which the sources cited in an AI answer are spread across different domains, publishers and source types rather than concentrated in a few recurring sites.

In short: How spread out the sources behind an AI answer are.

Why it matters

Diversity tells you how hard the category is to enter. Where answers draw on two or three dominant domains, displacing one is difficult but decisive. Where answers draw on many sources, entry is easier and the marginal citation is worth less.

How to work with it

For each prompt cluster, list the cited domains and count how often each recurs, then rank the domains you could realistically earn a citation from.

Common mistakes

  • Counting citations without looking at which domains hold them.
  • Ignoring review sites and directories that mediate the category.
  • Assuming a diverse citation set means any page can be cited.

Real example

In one software category, 68 percent of citations pointed to three domains: a review marketplace, a documentation hub and one publication. The remaining sources split across dozens of sites, each earning a citation in under 2 percent of prompts.

How to measure it

Build a domain-level citation table per prompt cluster, then ask which of those domains you can realistically earn a place on: your own site, partners, or earned media.

Where it breaks

High diversity does not mean easy entry. It can mean the category is fragmented and no single source carries authority, so citations produce little influence.

How it differs from adjacent terms

Citation Rate is your own share of citations; source diversity describes the whole field they come from. Compared with Competitive Citation Share, diversity is structural while share is positional: diversity tells you how hard entry is, share tells you where you stand. A category with low diversity offers durable returns and a long path to entry.

Quick checklist

  • Domain-level citation table built per prompt cluster.
  • Source types split: own site, marketplaces, media, standards.
  • Recurring domains ranked by realistic accessibility.
  • Marketplaces assessed as partners as well as competitors.
  • Change in diversity tracked across quarters.

FAQ

Low diversity: good or bad for a new brand?

Bad for entry, good for payoff. If only a few domains are cited consistently, earning a place among them takes longer but produces durable visibility.

How do I increase my share of a concentrated citation set?

Target the specific pages that already earn citations and out-specify them: more current data, clearer structure, stronger entity signals, and claims the incumbents do not make.
