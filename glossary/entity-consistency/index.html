What is Entity Consistency?

Last updated November 2026 · Reviewed against the IndexHot GEO Measurement Standard v1.0

Entity consistency means a brand presents one identity across its site, profiles, directories and press mentions. Inconsistency produces fragmentation: several weak entities instead of one strong one, none of which the system can confirm.

In short: Using the same name, description, category and identifiers for a brand everywhere a machine might look.

What has to match

  • Legal and trading name, including whether the brand is written with a space, a hyphen or a suffix.
  • One-sentence description of what the company does, reused rather than rewritten per platform.
  • Category vocabulary, since the category is how a system decides which comparison you belong in.
  • Identifiers: domain, logo, founding year, headquarters, and the profiles linked from sameAs.

Why small differences matter

A brand that appears as Acme, Acme Labs and Acme Software across three profiles looks like three related companies of uncertain size. A system resolving that has to hedge, and a hedged answer rarely recommends.

The same applies to product names. A product renamed on the site but not on review platforms produces two entities, and the older one, with reviews attached, usually wins.

Where inconsistency usually hides

  • Old press releases and directory entries nobody has updated since launch.
  • Localised sites with translated brand names and no sameAs links between them.
  • Sub-brands that reuse the parent name in a way that reads as a product line.
  • Social profiles registered by an agency and never handed over.

A maintenance routine

Keep a single source-of-truth record: name, one sentence, category, identifiers, profiles. Then audit the ten places a buyer or a crawler is most likely to look, annually, and fix the differences rather than the site copy.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as entity clarity?

Clarity is whether a system can tell what you are; consistency is whether the signals agree. You need both, and consistency is the cheaper half.

Should renamed products keep old names?

Keep the old name as a documented alias so records and reviews remain attached.

Does sameAs have to be complete?

It has to be accurate. A broken or dormant profile in the list is worse than a shorter list.
